TOP PICK 51 OF 75 · PERU
Mild coastal capital, easy rentista visa, taxed dividends
Peru has no wealth or inheritance tax, and its rentista status runs indefinitely on $1,000 a month of foreign income. Lima has good private clinics in Miraflores and San Isidro and a mild, almost rainless climate. The cost for BBD is income tax: residents' foreign dividends and interest are taxed at up to 30%, with no US income tax treaty and no newcomer relief.

THE ESSENTIALS
TAX ON HOLDINGS
No wealth tax; residents (183+ days in 12 months) add net foreign-source income, including US dividends and interest, to work income at progressive rates of 8–30%, with 30% above 45 UIT (about $72k; 2026), a foreign tax credit capped at Peru's average rate, no US income tax treaty and no newcomer regime.
ESTATE & INHERITANCE
Peru has no inheritance tax (a secondary source; PwC lists none), and inheritances are also outside the property transfer tax; there is no US estate treaty, and US estate tax applies above the $15M exemption (2026).
LENDING & BROKERS
Interactive Brokers reportedly serves all non-sanctioned countries, so Peru residents should be accepted, but margin terms are unconfirmed; Schwab One International's application reportedly lists Peru (October 2026), with no known margin restriction outside Europe.
RESIDENCY
Rentista residence on permanent income of at least $1,000/month from a pension or other foreign income, indefinite while the income continues (Legislative Decree 1350) and reportedly with no right to work; an investor route exists, but its amount is unverified.
HEALTHCARE
Private clinics such as Clínica Anglo Americana, Clínica Internacional, Clínica San Felipe and Clínica Delgado give good care; public EsSalud's voluntary "+Salud" plan (PEN 215/month at 60+) is closed to new members until the end of 2027, and new private plans get harder to buy after 65 (one forum report says Pacífico takes new members to 65).
SAFETY
Peru is at US State Dept Level 2 and "deteriorating" (September 2026, secondary source), with Level 4 for VRAEM and several Andean and Amazon areas; the UK reports gunpoint robberies in Miraflores and Barranco, fake taxis and express kidnappings, and protests that can escalate quickly.
CLIMATE
Mild and nearly rainless: sunny, muggy summers (December–April, highs 25–30°C) and grey, cool winters (June–October, highs 16–19°C) under low cloud and morning garúa drizzle; earthquake risk.
COMMUNITY
A modest expat community, concentrated in Miraflores, Barranco and San Isidro, with a growing number of international restaurants but no large US-retiree enclave; Spanish is needed.
GETTING THERE
From St. Louis (STL): no nonstop; one stop via Cancún or Atlanta, about 9–12 h.
Jorge Chávez, in Callao west of the centre, opened a new terminal in June 2025 and is the hub for Cusco and the rest of Peru; Lima traffic can make the ride to Miraflores long.
Routes as of Nov 2026; flying times are estimates.
WORTH KNOWING
Foreign dividends and interest taxed at up to 30%, with no treaty and no newcomer holiday
Grey, overcast winters for about half the year
Street crime in the expat districts, chronic political instability and earthquake risk

TOP PICK 42 OF 75 · CHILE
Andean capital with top clinics and a three-year tax window
Chile taxes a newly arrived foreigner only on Chilean-source income for the first three years, and a foreigner's assets abroad bought with non-Chilean money fall outside its inheritance tax. There is no wealth tax. Santiago has strong private clinics, led by Clínica Alemana and is 52nd in the 2026 Global Peace Index. After year three, though, US dividends are taxed at up to 40%, so a growth-tilted portfolio matters.

THE ESSENTIALS
TAX ON HOLDINGS
No wealth tax; a foreigner who takes up residence is taxed only on Chilean-source income for 3 years from entry, extendable in qualified cases, then worldwide, with US dividends and interest in the progressive Global Complementario at up to 40% (only real interest is taxed, and a US–Chile income tax treaty exists).
ESTATE & INHERITANCE
Heirs pay 1–25% on their share, but under Law 16.271 art. 1 a foreigner's assets abroad count only if bought with money from Chile, so a US brokerage account funded from the US falls outside it; whether this holds for a foreigner who dies domiciled in Chile is unverified, and there is no US estate treaty.
LENDING & BROKERS
Interactive Brokers reportedly serves all non-sanctioned countries, so Chile residents should be accepted, but margin terms are unconfirmed; Schwab One International's application reportedly lists Chile (October 2026), with no known margin restriction outside Europe.
RESIDENCY
Temporary residence as a rentista or pensionado has no fixed minimum, though applicants are reportedly advised to show more than about $1,000/month; the permit is renewable, and permanent residence needs 24 months of temporary residence (12 in some cases, such as pension income), longer if absences total more than 2 months (Migration Service, 2026).
HEALTHCARE
Clínica Alemana (1st in Chile and 121st in the world in Newsweek's 2026 ranking), Clínica Las Condes (5th in Chile), Clínica Santa María (9th) and Clínica Indisa (12th) lead private care; retirees with no Chilean taxable income can qualify for free public FONASA Group A, while private Isapres price steeply by age and can exclude declared conditions for about 18 months.
SAFETY
Chile is 52nd of 163 in the 2026 Global Peace Index and US State Dept Level 2 (August 2026, secondary source); the UK reports common and increasingly armed muggings, pickpocketing and carjackings in affluent areas, and expats favour Providencia, Las Condes, Vitacura and Ñuñoa.
CLIMATE
Mediterranean at about 570 m: dry summers with January highs about 30°C, cool winters with July highs about 16°C and lows near 4°C, and most of the roughly 290 mm of rain May–August; winter smog in the valley and earthquake risk.
COMMUNITY
A lively, mostly working-age expat scene centred on the east-side communes (Providencia, Las Condes, Vitacura, Ñuñoa), with meetups and online groups but no large retiree enclave; Spanish is needed day to day.
GETTING THERE
From St. Louis (STL): no nonstop; one stop via Cancún or Atlanta, about 12–15 h.
Arturo Merino Benítez (SCL) is about 17 km north-west of the centre; the UK advises booking official taxis or transfers inside the terminal.
Routes as of Nov 2026; flying times are estimates.
WORTH KNOWING
After three years, US dividends are taxed at up to 40%
Winter smog, and earthquake risk
Rising armed street crime, and costs high for the region

TOP PICK 17 OF 75 · URUGUAY
Stable capital with an 11-year tax holiday on foreign income
Uruguay is 13th in International Living's 2026 index and 43rd in the 2026 Global Peace Index, the highest in Latin America. New residents can exempt foreign capital income for the arrival year plus 10, and there is no inheritance tax. Montevideo is a calm, mid-priced capital with good private health cooperatives. It is far from the US, its homicide rate has been rising, and the holiday needs 183 or more days a year there unless you make a large qualifying investment.

THE ESSENTIALS
TAX ON HOLDINGS
Wealth tax of 0.1% (2026) reaches only net wealth located in Uruguay, so a US brokerage account is outside it; under Law 20.446 (from 2026) new residents with 183+ days' presence can exempt foreign capital income for the arrival year plus 10, then pay 6% for 5 years and 12% after (that it covers dividends and interest is inferred from "capital income"; the older 7% option is reportedly closed to new residents).
ESTATE & INHERITANCE
Uruguay has no inheritance tax, so heirs owe nothing locally on US assets; there is no US estate treaty, and US estate tax applies above the $15M exemption (2026).
LENDING & BROKERS
Interactive Brokers reportedly accepts Uruguay residents, and Schwab One International's application reportedly lists Uruguay (October 2026), with no known margin restriction outside Europe; IBKR margin terms by country are unconfirmed.
RESIDENCY
Permanent residency as a rentista on income from abroad needs a notarial certificate of its nature and monthly amount, with no published minimum (officials reportedly expect about $1,500/month, and dividends qualify as passive income); processing can take 6–18 months, and you are legal while it is pending.
HEALTHCARE
Private nonprofit cooperatives (mutualistas such as Hospital Británico, Asociación Española, Médica Uruguaya, CASMU) give full coverage for about $90–140 per adult a month (2026), with age-based admission rules and senior surcharges; the public ASSE serves the rest, and Montevideo holds most specialty care.
SAFETY
Uruguay is 43rd in the 2026 Global Peace Index and reportedly at US State Dept Level 2, but Montevideo's homicide rate was about 16 per 100,000 in 2025 and the first half of 2026 had Uruguay's most homicides since 2015; Pocitos, Punta Carretas and Carrasco are the calmer areas.
CLIMATE
Temperate: warm, humid summers (December–March) and cool, damp, windy winters around 10°C.
COMMUNITY
A small but steady expat community, mostly Argentine and European; fewer US retirees and English speakers than the Mexican or Panamanian hubs.
GETTING THERE
From St. Louis (STL): two stops, for example via Tampa and Bogotá, about 15–19 h.
Carrasco airport (MVD) is about 20 km east of the centre; ferries cross the Río de la Plata to Buenos Aires, and São Paulo, Panama City and Bogotá are common connecting hubs.
Routes as of Nov 2026; flying times are estimates.
WORTH KNOWING
The tax holiday needs 183+ days a year in Uruguay (or a large investment), and after 11 years foreign income is taxed at 6%, then 12%
Rising violent crime in Montevideo despite Uruguay's national peace ranking
Very few nonstop flights to the US, and none for most of the year
