AE
PROFILE · UNITED ARAB EMIRATES
No income, wealth or inheritance tax, in a desert heat and a tense region
Dubai has no personal income tax, no wealth tax and no inheritance tax, a five-year renewable retirement visa from age 55 on AED 1M of property or savings, strong private hospitals and nonstops to a dozen US cities, which on paper makes it a near-ideal buy-borrow-die base. What stands against it: summers above 40°C, costly private health insurance that rises steeply after 60, no path to permanent residence, and a US Level 3 "reconsider travel" advisory since March 2026 over the conflict with Iran.

THE ESSENTIALS
TAX ON HOLDINGS
No personal income tax, no wealth tax and no capital gains tax: dividends, interest and gains are untaxed locally; a US citizen still files and pays US tax on worldwide income, and there is no US–UAE income tax treaty.
ESTATE & INHERITANCE
No inheritance, estate or gift tax; since 2023 a non-Muslim foreigner's estate follows the civil personal status law (or home-country law, if chosen) rather than Sharia, and a will registered with the DIFC Wills Service Centre is the usual safeguard; there is no US–UAE estate treaty, so the $15M US exemption (2026) is the only relief.
LENDING & BROKERS
IBKR reportedly serves UAE residents (many fund accounts through a dirham or US-dollar account), and Schwab One International's online form reportedly accepted the UAE in Oct 2026; margin by country of residence is unreported, and Fidelity and Vanguard reportedly open no new accounts for residents abroad.
RESIDENCY
Retirement residence permit: 55+ with at least 15 years of prior work (the GDRFA page lists both), plus property worth AED 1M (about $272k), an AED 1M deposit in a UAE bank, or a fixed income of AED 240,000/year (about $65k); five years, renewable on the same terms, with no route to permanent residence or citizenship; an AED 2M property buyer can instead apply for a golden visa.
HEALTHCARE
Good private hospitals, such as American Hospital Dubai, reportedly JCI-accredited; health insurance is compulsory for Dubai residents, there are no retiree-specific plans, and premiums climb with age and claims: basic narrow-network plans start at about AED 5,000/year, a 67-year-old retiree paid just over AED 25,000/year for a basic policy, and insurers may load up to 100% per pre-existing condition (2024).
SAFETY
The US has rated the UAE Level 3, "reconsider travel", for terrorism and armed conflict since early March 2026, when the US urged citizens to leave during the US and Israeli strikes on Iran (summary updated October 2026 for possible Houthi attacks); street crime is very low, but local laws are strict, with arrest or exit bans for offences that would be minor at home.
CLIMATE
Hot desert: August highs about 40°C (104°F) with very humid nights near 30°C, so from about May to September life moves indoors; January highs about 24°C, and under 100 mm of rain a year.
COMMUNITY
About 92% of Dubai's nearly 4 million people are expatriates, with over 100,000 Britons the largest Western group and English the everyday working language; the population is young and working, and retirees are few.
GETTING THERE
From St. Louis (STL): no nonstop; one stop via Chicago or Toronto, about 17–20 h.
Dubai International (DXB), one of the world's busiest hubs, is close to the old centre and linked by metro; flights were suspended during the 2026 conflict and restored by May 2026, and schedules can still change at short notice.
Routes as of Nov 2026; flying times are estimates.
WORTH KNOWING
Summers above 40°C for about five months
US Level 3 advisory since March 2026 over the regional conflict with Iran
Private health insurance that rises steeply with age, and no permanent residence

PROFILE · SOUTH AFRICA
Spectacular and cheap, but crime and South Africa's taxes at death
Cape Town pairs mountains, ocean and wine country with a Mediterranean climate, very good private hospitals, English as an everyday language, low costs and US nonstops, and its retired person visa accepts income from a portfolio. For buy-borrow-die it is a poor fit: South Africa taxes residents on worldwide income, treats death as a sale (capital gains tax on gains made while resident, which defeats the US step-up), adds 20–25% estate duty on a worldwide estate and charges an exit tax on leaving, and violent crime is high.

THE ESSENTIALS
TAX ON HOLDINGS
No wealth tax; residents are taxed on worldwide income: most foreign dividends at an effective 20%, foreign interest at marginal rates up to 45%, and 40% of capital gains included in income (at most 18%); the US treaty credit covers only the 15% US tax on dividends, so for someone in a 15% US bracket dividends cost about 5% more; leaving triggers an exit tax on worldwide assets (s9H, at most 18% on the gain).
ESTATE & INHERITANCE
Estate duty of 20% above an R3.5M abatement (25% above R30M) on an ordinarily resident decedent's worldwide estate, plus capital gains tax at death on gains made while resident (death is a deemed disposal); foreign assets owned before first becoming resident are deductible (s4(e)), but how that holds for a portfolio rebalanced over decades is unclear; the US–South Africa estate treaty reportedly gives credits only, so below the $15M US exemption (2026) the duty applies in full.
LENDING & BROKERS
IBKR reportedly accepts South Africa residents (Oct 2026 review), while Schwab One International reportedly does not; margin by country of residence is unreported, and Fidelity and Vanguard reportedly open no new accounts for residents abroad.
RESIDENCY
Retired person visa: at least R37,000/month (about $2,200) from a pension, annuity or retirement account, or from a net worth or combination of assets producing that income; up to 4 years and renewable, with permanent residence on the same income (s27(e)) or on R12M net worth plus a one-off R120,000 payment (financial independence, s27(f)); a White Paper approved by Cabinet in April 2026 would add a minimum age and a higher income, not yet law.
HEALTHCARE
Very good private care (UK advice: "a very high standard of private medical care") at hospitals such as Netcare Christiaan Barnard Memorial on the Foreshore, while public care varies; local medical schemes cannot refuse on age but add a late-joiner penalty of up to 75% of the contribution for someone with no prior South African cover, so a new 70-year-old pays roughly R16,000–17,000/month (about $1,000) for comprehensive cover (secondary source).
SAFETY
The US rates South Africa Level 2, "exercise increased caution", for crime, terrorism, unrest and kidnapping, and requires its staff to get permission for many townships around Cape Town; UK advice reports violent crime mostly in townships and informal settlements, attacks on the airport roads (use the M3 and N2, not the R300) and muggings of hikers in Table Mountain National Park.
CLIMATE
Mediterranean: dry, warm summers (January–February highs about 27°C) and mild, wet winters (July about 18°C by day, 7°C at night), about 500 mm of rain a year, and a strong south-easterly wind in spring and summer; a severe drought nearly ran the city dry in 2015–18.
COMMUNITY
English is the first language of about 28% of Capetonians (with Afrikaans and Xhosa) and is widely used in business and daily life, so a newcomer manages in English; the size of the foreign retiree community is not well documented.
GETTING THERE
From St. Louis (STL): no nonstop; one stop via Atlanta or Washington, about 19–22 h.
Cape Town International is about 20 km east of the centre; many long-haul trips connect through Johannesburg, about 2 hours' flight away.
Routes as of Nov 2026; flying times are estimates.
WORTH KNOWING
High violent crime, including carjacking and attacks on the airport roads and mountain trails
Capital gains tax at death and 20–25% estate duty on the worldwide estate
Worldwide income tax while resident and an exit tax on leaving

TOP PICK 38 OF 75 · MONTENEGRO
Adriatic fjord town with residence through a €150k home
Montenegro has no wealth tax, taxes investment income at a flat 15% and levies inheritance tax only on real estate, so a US portfolio passes to children untaxed. Buying a €150k property gives temporary residence, and Kotor's walled old town sits on one of the Adriatic's most striking bays, but healthcare is thin.

THE ESSENTIALS
TAX ON HOLDINGS
No wealth tax; residents are taxed on worldwide income, with dividends and interest at 15% of the gross amount and capital gains at 15% of the gain, and Kotor's 13% municipal surtax on income tax may raise that to about 17% (whether it applies to capital income is unverified); there is no newcomer regime.
ESTATE & INHERITANCE
Inheritance tax of 3–6% applies only to inherited real estate, and close family are reportedly exempt, so a US portfolio passes to US-resident children with no Montenegrin tax; there is no US estate treaty.
LENDING & BROKERS
IBKR reportedly accepts residents of all non-sanctioned countries, including Montenegro, though margin terms per country are unknown; Schwab International most likely does not accept Montenegro, which is off its reported list; as a non-EU country, EU PRIIPs limits on US ETFs should not apply, though which IBKR entity carries the account is unconfirmed.
RESIDENCY
Temporary residence by owning real estate with a Tax Authority-assessed value of at least €150,000 (since January 2026; older permits are grandfathered), plus means of subsistence and health insurance; renewed yearly, and the time reportedly does not count toward permanent residence.
HEALTHCARE
Kotor's hospital covers the bay, with complex cases sent to the Clinical Centre in Podgorica or to Croatia and Serbia; the public system is stretched by newcomers, private clinics are few, and health insurance is a condition of residence.
SAFETY
Montenegro is 30th in the 2026 Global Peace Index; UK travel advice warns of pickpockets in tourist spots and of violence between organised crime groups that can affect bystanders.
CLIMATE
Hot summers (highs about 31°C in July and August) and mild but very wet winters: about 1,400 mm of rain a year, mostly from November to January, with January lows near 3°C.
COMMUNITY
A small but growing foreign community along the bay and in nearby Tivat and Budva (remote workers, retirees, property buyers); outside the tourist trade, English is less common.
GETTING THERE
From St. Louis (STL): two stops, for example via Chicago and Istanbul, about 17–21 h.
Tivat airport is 5 km away and busiest in summer, and Podgorica (65 km) is open year-round; Dubrovnik's airport, with wider service, is about 90 minutes' drive across the Croatian border.
Routes as of Nov 2026; flying times are estimates.
WORTH KNOWING
Weak healthcare: serious care means Podgorica or another country
Cruise-ship crowds in summer and heavy winter rain
The property permit renews yearly and builds no path to permanent residence
