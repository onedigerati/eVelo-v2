01
Buy, borrow, die

A way to live off a portfolio without selling it.

Buy assets that grow and hold them. Borrow against them for living money, with a securities-backed line of credit, instead of selling shares and paying capital-gains tax each time. The loan grows with interest; the portfolio, untouched, keeps compounding. Die holding them: under US law your heirs inherit the shares with a stepped-up cost basis, their value on the day you die, so the gains you borrowed against are never taxed. The estate repays the loan and passes on the rest.

It works when the portfolio grows faster than the loan. It fails in one specific way, and that is what eVelo is built to measure.

The failure mode of Buy-Borrow-Die is not a bad year. It is being forced to sell in one.
02
What “holds” means

Every simulated future ends in one of three ways.

Holds

The loan never reaches the lender’s margin-call line and the portfolio is still worth more than the loan at the end of your horizon. This is the headline number.

Bruised

At least one margin call forced a sale, usually after a crash, but the plan got through. You survived; you also sold at a bad time and paid tax to do it.

Broken

The debt caught up with the assets: the loan reached the portfolio’s value, or a margin call could not be met even by selling everything.
