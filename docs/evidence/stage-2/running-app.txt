## F. Method page, "Living abroad"
method text: A typed number is read as a real price you found, not as your final spend, so the premium still applies on top. It is pinned to the premium it was typed at, and when the slider moves it scales the way eVelo’s own estimate for that category and city moves:value = typed × m(e) ÷ m(e₀), capped at the larger of the home amount (or 100%) and what was typedm(e) = r + (1 − r) × e, eVelo’s own ratio for that category and city; e₀ = the premium the value was typed at, e = the premium now.For example, $6,000 a year of housing in Bangkok, typed at a 35% premium, reads $6,512 at 65%: the model’s housing ratio rises from 84% to 92% of St. Louis’s. A typed value reads exactly as typed at its own premium and returns to it when the slider comes back. Categories the premium doesn’t touch (dearer than home, and travel) never move. Changing the home budget doesn’t move a typed amount either, unless it sits at the cap: then a lower home budget lowers it, never below what was typed.
