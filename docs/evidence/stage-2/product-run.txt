check4 method has "If you have already borrowed": Buy assets that grow and hold them. Borrow against them for living money, with a securities-backed line of credit, instead of selling shares and paying capital-gains tax each time. The loan grows with interest; the portfolio, untouched, keeps compounding. Die holding them: under US law your heirs inherit the shares with a stepped-up cost basis, their value on the day you die, so the gains you borrowed against are never taxed. The estate repays the loan and passes on the rest. If you have already borrowed, eVelo starts from that balance.
check4 method has "The loan starts at any balance": Interest capitalizes. The loan grows by a twelfth of its annual rate (6% by default). You never pay interest out of pocket; it is added to the balance and compounds. The loan starts at any balance you have already drawn (zero if none), which accrues at the same rate.
check4 method has "If you have an existing loan balance": Every future also runs a second, simpler plan on the same markets: sell as you go. Each month it sells enough shares to cover spending, paying capital-gains tax on the gain in each share sold, and carries no loan. If you have an existing loan balance, this plan starts with the portfolio minus that balance and no debt: had you sold instead, those shares would already be spent, and both plans start from the same net worth.
