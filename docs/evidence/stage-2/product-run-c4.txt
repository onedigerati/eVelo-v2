c4 method (500k): A way to live off a portfolio without selling it.
c4 method (500k): Buy assets that grow and hold them. Borrow against them for living money, with a securities-backed line of credit, instead of selling shares and paying capital-gains tax each time. The loan grows with interest; the portfolio, untouched, keeps compounding. Die holding them: under US law your heirs inherit the shares with a stepped-up cost basis, their value on the day you die, so the gains you borrowed against are never taxed. The estate repays the loan and passes on the rest. If you have already borrowed, eVelo starts from that balance.
c4 method (500k): The debt caught up with the assets: the loan reached the portfolio’s value, or a margin call could not be met even by selling everything.
c4 method (500k): The key is that a whole month is drawn at once. When the future borrows October 2008, every holding gets its October 2008 return and inflation gets October 2008’s CPI. Crashes stay as deep as they were, recoveries as long, and the way your holdings fall together (or don’t) is preserved without estimating a single correlation. The portfolio is rebalanced to your weights every month, and a 0.3% a year drag stands in for tax on dividends.
c4 method (500k): The rest of the weight goes to the prior: a 3.5% risk-free rate plus, for stocks and stock funds, beta times an equity premium of 5% (balanced) or 3.5% (conservative), with beta held between 0.5 and 2. Bonds and gold get the risk-free rate plus 0.5%; crypto gets it plus 6%. The whole series is then shifted to the blended average, so its volatility and its crashes are untouched. The “historical” outlook skips this and uses each asset’s real average as is.
c4 method (500k): Interest capitalizes. The loan grows by a twelfth of its annual rate (6% by default). You never pay interest out of pocket; it is added to the balance and compounds. The loan starts at any balance you have already drawn (zero if none), which accrues at the same rate. If that balance is already above the margin-call line, the lender forces a sale today, before the first month: it brings the loan to the target and pays capital-gains tax, like any forced sale below, and the odds are judged from there. That opening sale isn’t counted as a margin call. A balance the sale can’t cover after tax fails at once.
c4 method (500k): You draw what you need. This month’s spending, grown with inflation, minus any other income (Social Security, a pension) is borrowed. If income exceeds spending, the surplus pays the loan down first.
c4 method (500k): The lender checks. If the loan has reached the portfolio’s value, the future is broken. If the loan-to-value ratio is above the margin-call line (60% by default), there is a forced sale.
c4 method (500k): A forced sale sells just enough to bring the ratio back to the target (45% by default). Each dollar sold carries some unrealized gain, and the tax on that gain (23.8% by default, the top federal long-term rate including the 3.8% investment-income tax) comes out of the proceeds before they reach the lender:
c4 method (500k): If even selling everything could not reach the target, the future counts as broken, not bruised. The margin-call chart on the Outlook shows when, across all futures, the first forced sale tends to happen.
c4 method (500k): Wealth tax, every year. At each year-end after the move, the marginal bands are applied to your real net worth (portfolio minus loan) or, where the law taxes the gross value (Italy’s IVAFE), to the portfolio. Because the bands are in today’s dollars, the thresholds hold steady in real terms. The borrow plan adds the tax to the loan, before that month’s margin check; the sell plan sells shares to pay it, with capital-gains tax on the sale.
c4 method (500k): Every future also runs a second, simpler plan on the same markets: sell as you go. Each month it sells enough shares to cover spending, paying capital-gains tax on the gain in each share sold, and carries no loan. If you have an existing loan balance, this plan starts with the portfolio minus that balance and no debt: had you sold instead, those shares would already be spent, and both plans start from the same net worth. (It ignores the tax and interest already paid, which roughly offset.) If the balance forces an opening sale, its tax falls on the borrow plan only, so borrowing starts that much behind. c4 method (500k): Borrow: the portfolio minus the loan. With the step-up in basis, no tax is due on the gains at death.
c4 method (500k): Sell: whatever portfolio is left, having paid tax along the way.
c4 method (500k): Borrowing wins when the portfolio’s return beats the loan rate: the tax you never paid keeps compounding. It loses when rates are high, returns are low, or margin calls force sales at the bottom. Both estates are after any local inheritance tax. The Outlook reports both medians and the share of futures in which borrowing comes out ahead.
