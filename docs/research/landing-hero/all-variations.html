Variation B

Pick your city

The race chart is the hero: tap a city and the loan line glides to that city's spending, while the odds and what your heirs inherit at 90 update beside it.

Best for visitors who learn by poking; the geographic lever is one tap away. Cost to build medium: one SVG chart, a tween, a radiogroup. Risk a single median path can read as a promise; the odds chip carries the uncertainty.

Variation C

Scroll story

The hero hands off to a scrollytelling section: four short panels scroll past one sticky chart, and the chart advances a step each time a panel reaches the middle of the screen.

Best for visitors who scroll; it teaches the whole mechanism in four beats with no clicks. Cost to build the most work of the set: a sticky layout, scroll observers, a tweened chart and a phone layout of its own. Risk the riskiest on phones: the sticky chart takes nearly half the screen, mobile address bars shift the trigger line, and a fast flick can skip a beat.

Illustrative numbers · one typical future, not a forecast

01 · Buy

Buy, and never sell.

Start with $2.5M of shares. You paid $1.0M for them, so $1.5M is gain you would be taxed on if you sold.

You don't sell. At 7% a year the shares compound to about $19.0M by age 90, and the hatched gains are never taxed.

02 · Borrow

Borrow your living money.

Each year you draw what you spend from a credit line secured by the shares: $80k in Austin, rising 3% a year. Interest at 6% is added to the loan.

The loan grows, but the shares grow faster. The lender calls when the loan passes half the portfolio. In Austin the loan ends at 49%, almost touching that line.

03 · Live anywhere

Spend it where life costs less.

The same life in Lisbon costs about $50k a year, 38% less. A smaller draw every year bends the loan flatter: $5.9M at 90 instead of $9.4M.

The loan stays far below the margin-call line. Across many futures, that is 88% of plans holding in Lisbon against 63% in Austin.

04 · Die

Pass it on, gains untaxed.

At death the shares step up to their market value, so $18.0M of gains are never taxed.

The estate repays the loan from the portfolio. Your heirs keep the rest: about $13.2M.

Variation D
