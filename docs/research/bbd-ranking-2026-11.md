# BBD destinations: ranking research (2026-11-06)

Stage 1 researched 23 countries and drafted a top 25. Stage 2 added 11 countries (Appendices F–G) and city-level notes (Appendix H), and drafted a top 50. Stage 7 added 7 countries (Appendices I–J) and 13 retiree towns (Appendix K), and drafted the top 75 below.

**Who this is for:** a US citizen retiring abroad on buy-borrow-die (BBD).
- A taxable US brokerage portfolio is never sold.
- Spending comes from margin or securities-based loans against it.
- The estate passes to heirs with a US step-up in basis.

**What matters, in order:**
1. A wealth tax on the portfolio, paid every year.
2. Inheritance tax at death, and whether a US treaty gives relief.
3. Tax on dividends and interest. Capital gains matter little while nothing is sold.
4. A residency route that accepts investment income or assets, not only a pension.
5. Whether a US broker will keep lending to someone living there.
6. Healthcare, and private insurance that can still be bought into the late 70s.

These sit alongside the existing factors: cost, safety, climate and flights.

**How it was researched:** five research passes (Iberia/France, Med/Balkans, Latin America, Asia, and cross-cutting facts). Each fact cites a page that was fetched and read.
- Sources are marked *secondary* or *unverified* where that applies. The full sourced notes are Appendices A–E.
- The coordinator re-read four key claims against their cited pages:
  - Malta: the new 2027 rules and the €35k minimum (Deloitte).
  - Cyprus: the non-dom exemption on dividends and interest (PwC).
  - Valencia: the €2M wealth-tax exemption from the 2026 assessment (Cuatrecasas).
  - Uruguay: 11 years tax-free on foreign capital income, then 5 years at 6% (IMI Daily).
- Corrections made from those re-reads:
  - Malta: Deloitte says applications filed by 31 Dec 2026 keep the old terms to 2031. It does not literally say the Global Residence Programme "closes".
  - Cyprus: the "17 of 20 years" domicile limit is not on that PwC page; it rests on the other Cyprus sources in Appendix B.
  - Uruguay: the holiday is stated for "foreign-sourced capital income"; that it covers dividends and interest specifically is an inference.

## Facts that apply everywhere

- **US estate tax follows the citizen.** The 2026 exemption is $15,000,000 per person (P.L. 119-21), with no sunset and inflation indexing after 2026. The §1014 step-up applies wherever the person lived. https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill ; https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/2010 ; https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1014 (Appendix E §2)
- **Treaties.** US estate/gift tax treaties exist with only **France, Greece and Italy** among the candidates. https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-gift-tax-treaties-international
- **Without a treaty, a local inheritance tax on US stocks is double tax.** The §2014 credit covers only foreign death tax on property sited in that country, and US stocks are US-sited. So countries that tax heirs on worldwide assets (Ecuador, Colombia, the Philippines, France without its treaty, some Spanish regions) are the real hazard for the "die" step. (Appendix E §2)
- **Lending abroad is the weakest-sourced factor.** The broker sites (IBKR, Schwab International) refused automated fetches, so this rests on secondary pages that were read; Appendix E §1 has the table.
  - Fidelity and Vanguard open no accounts for people living abroad.
  - Merrill closed expat accounts in 2016.
  - Interactive Brokers (IBKR) reportedly serves all non-sanctioned countries; whether margin is allowed per country is unknown.
  - Schwab One International's online form (checked by a third party in Oct 2026) accepts PT, ES, GR, MX, CR, PA, CO, EC, UY, MY, TH and ID. It does not accept FR, IT, HR, MT, DR or VN, and probably not CY, ME or BZ.
  - Schwab reportedly bars Europe-resident customers from taking cash out through margin.
  - **Practical read:** in Europe, plan on IBKR margin. In Latin America and Asia, Schwab International is a second option.
- **Health insurance age caps.** International cover still available into the late 70s, and the binding constraint for later movers. (Appendix E §3; Appendix C)
  - Cigna Global has no upper entry age and guarantees lifetime renewal on its Senior plan. It costs about $6.5–9.4k a year at 70–79, worldwide excluding the US.
  - Bupa Global reportedly has no upper limit.
  - GeoBlue, IMG and APRIL stop new entrants at 70–74.

For this profile the biggest costs are wealth tax, tax on dividends and interest, and inheritance tax on the worldwide estate. Capital gains tax barely matters while nothing is sold.

### Portugal (Algarve, Lisbon, Porto, Madeira)

- **Wealth tax:** none. PwC says Portugal has no net wealth or net worth tax, so financial assets in Portugal or abroad are not taxed. There is a municipal property tax on registered real-estate value. The additional property tax (AIMI) on high-value Portuguese real estate exists but is not covered by the fetched page (unverified here). https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/portugal/individual/other-taxes
- **Inheritance/estate tax:** there is no inheritance tax as such. Instead a 10% stamp duty applies to free acquisitions (inheritance or gift), plus 0.8% on gifts of property. Spouse/partner, children, grandchildren and parents are exempt. The duty covers assets located in Portugal, whoever the deceased or heir is. Foreign-company shares are not addressed explicitly, but US-situs brokerage assets passing to children appear untaxed in any case. There is **no US–Portugal estate treaty**. Forced-heirship succession rules for residents are a separate civil-law issue. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/portugal/individual/other-taxes ; https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/global/tax/practical-tax-guide-to-inheritance-and-gifts-received-in-portugal ; https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-gift-tax-treaties-international
- **Investment income tax:** residents are taxed on worldwide income. Progressive rates for 2026 are 12.5%–48%, plus a 2.5%/5% solidarity surcharge above €80k/€250k.
  - Dividends and interest pay a **28% flat rate**, or you can opt to aggregate them with other income at progressive rates. A foreign tax credit applies, limited by the treaty rate, so the 15% US withholding is creditable.
  - Capital gains also pay 28% flat. For listed securities held more than 2, 5 or 8 years, 10%, 20% or 30% of the gain is excluded. Gains on assets held under 365 days must be aggregated when taxable income is at least €86,634.
  - Income from blacklisted jurisdictions pays 35% (the US is not blacklisted).
  - **NHR (the former non-habitual resident regime) is closed.** Its replacement, **IFICI**, gives a 20% rate on employment or business income from eligible research/innovation activities, and exempts most foreign income for people working in those activities. It is aimed at workers, not retirees or passive investors, and excludes former NHR beneficiaries.
  - No exit tax or deemed-gain charge on individuals' portfolios was found (not verified either way).
  - https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/portugal/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/portugal/individual/income-determination ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/portugal/individual/significant-developments
- **Residency route:** the **D7 (passive income) visa**.
  - Income required in 2026: **€920/month** for the main applicant (the minimum wage), plus 50% per extra adult (€1,380 for a couple) and 30% per child.
  - Accepted income includes dividends, fund income, interest, rent, pensions and annuities. Savings alone do not count, though 12 months of funds is also expected (€11,040 single, €16,560 couple).
  - Private health insurance is needed by the AIMA (immigration agency) appointment.
  - The 2027 budget assumes a €970 minimum wage, which is not yet law. One source says €920 is consular practice rather than statute.
  - Note: loan draws are not income. Show recurring dividends or interest.
  - https://www.portugalist.com/d7-visa-requirements/
- **Healthcare:**
  - **Public:** all residents can use the national health service (SNS) once registered with a user number (needs a residence permit and tax number). One source says it is free for over-65s, with small co-payments otherwise.
  - **Private:** most local plans close to new joiners over about 65. Some go to 80 or have no limit, and international insurers such as Cigna Global have no upper age.
  - **Cost:** fetched sources give only general figures (about €50–100/month for comprehensive plans, €400–1,000/year on average). Age-specific premiums for 65–75 were **not verified**. Search snippets suggested roughly €120–250+/month at older ages, and €500+ for international cover.
  - https://www.movingtoportugal.pt/en/daily-life/health-insurance/ ; https://expatica.com/pt/healthcare/healthcare-basics/elderly-care-portugal-2172956 ; https://www.savoryandpartners.com/blog/healthcare-portugal-guide
- **Special notes:** Madeira has its own lower VAT (22/12/4%). Its regional income-tax reductions apply to progressive rates, not the 28% flat rate (my understanding, not verified). https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/portugal/individual/other-taxes

**BBD verdict:** Very friendly. There is no wealth tax, and nothing is due at death for spouse or children (stamp duty is Portuguese-situs only and family are exempt). The real cost is 28% on dividends and interest (about 13% net after the US credit), with no special regime for retirees now that NHR is closed.

**City notes:** the rules are national, so Algarve, Lisbon and Porto are taxed the same. Madeira (an autonomous region) differs only on VAT and regional IRS tweaks.

### Spain (Valencia, Málaga, Tarragona)

- **Wealth tax:** the national wealth tax (Impuesto sobre el Patrimonio, IP) taxes residents on **worldwide net wealth**, including financial assets. Debts are deducted, so margin debt reduces the base.
  - The state default exemption is €700k, plus €300k for the main home. The state scale runs 0.2%–3.5%.
  - The **Solidarity Tax on Large Fortunes (ITSGF)** is now **permanent**. It charges 1.7%/2.1%/3.5% above €3M, with no tax in practice until about €3.7M. Regional wealth tax paid is credited against it.
  - **Combined cap:** income tax plus wealth tax cannot exceed **60% of the income-tax base**, but the wealth-tax reduction is capped at 80%. A BBD household with little taxable income therefore pays at least about 20% of its wealth-tax bill (my inference from the cap).
  - **By region:**
    - **Valencian Community (Valencia):** exemption raised to **€2,000,000** from the 31-12-2026 assessment (Ley 5/2026), no general relief, rates 0.25%–3.5%.
    - **Andalusia (Málaga):** 100% wealth-tax relief with the €700k exemption. Above about €3.7M, tax is collected only through regional wealth tax at rates matching the solidarity tax, so large fortunes still pay about 1.7–3.5%.
    - **Catalonia (Tarragona):** exemption only **€500,000**, rates 0.21%–3.48%, no relief. Very wealthy residents can owe both regional wealth tax and the solidarity tax.
  - https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/spain/individual/other-taxes ; https://www.cuatrecasas.com/es/spain/art/comunidad-valenciana-novedades-tributarias-2026 ; https://guiafiscal.es/opinion/patrimonio-2026-mapa-bonificaciones-ccaa/ ; https://www.cuatrecasas.com/es/spain/fiscalidad/art/itsgf-medidas-adoptadas-ccaa ; https://www.cuatrecasas.com/en/latam/tax/art/wt-pit-non-residents-benefit-wealth-tax-shield
- **Inheritance tax:**
  - The tax falls on the **heir**. Resident heirs are taxed on worldwide acquisitions; non-resident heirs only on Spanish-situs assets.
  - So US-resident children inheriting US brokerage assets from a Spain-resident parent appear to be **outside Spanish inheritance tax**. This is an inference from the heir-residence rule; confirm with a Spanish adviser.
  - The state scale is 7.65%–34%, with large regional reliefs. Which region's rules apply follows the deceased's habitual residence.
  - **For adult children:**
    - Andalusia: €1M reduction per heir plus a 99% relief on the tax.
    - Valencian Community: €100k reduction plus a 99% relief.
    - Catalonia: a sliding relief (spouse a fixed 99%; children on a scale that shrinks to about 20%) that thins out on large estates. Exact Catalan bands were not verified.
  - **No US–Spain estate treaty**, so any US estate tax gets credit only under US domestic law.
  - Gains at death are not subject to income tax.
  - https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/spain/individual/other-taxes ; https://www.taxesforexpats.com/country-guides/spain/succession-inheritance-tax-spain.html ; https://guiafiscal.es/actualidad/mapa-sucesiones-2026-donde-se-paga-mas/ ; https://www.recordinglaw.com/es/spain/inheritance-law/inheritance-tax-comparison/ ; https://www.costaluzlawyers.com/sucesiones-por-comunidad-autonoma-2026-andalucia-vs-cataluna-vs-madrid/ ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/spain/individual/income-determination
- **Investment income tax:** residents are taxed on worldwide income.
  - Dividends, interest and capital gains are "savings income," taxed at **19%** (first €6k), **21%** (to €50k), **23%** (to €200k), **27%** (to €300k) and **30%** above.
  - The **Beckham regime** (special expatriate regime) is for people moving to Spain to work, not retirees.
  - **Exit tax** (art. 95 bis): on leaving Spain after at least 10 of the prior 15 years as a resident, with shares worth more than €4M (or a stake over 25% in an entity worth more than €1M), unrealised gains are taxed. The charge is deferred, and lapses after 10 years without a triggering event, for moves within the EU/EEA. This is relevant to a long-term BBD holder who later leaves Spain.
  - https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/spain/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/spain/individual/income-determination ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/spain/individual/other-issues ; https://www.forvismazars.com/es/en/insights/latest-news/cambio-de-residencia-fiscal-en-el-irpf-espanol
- **Residency route:** the **non-lucrative visa**.
  - It requires 400% of the IPREM (Spain's benchmark income index): **€2,400/month, or €28,800/year**, plus €7,200/year per dependant (€36,000 for a couple). These figures use the 2026 IPREM of €600/month, which is not yet officially confirmed for 2026.
  - Liquid savings or investment income count.
  - It needs Spanish private health insurance (no co-pay is usual consular practice).
  - https://www.costaluzlawyers.com/es/iprem-2026-visado-no-lucrativo-espana/
- **Healthcare:**
  - **Public:** non-lucrative visa holders do not get public cover automatically. After **1 year** of residence and municipal registration they can buy into the public system through the **convenio especial**, at **€60/month under 65 or €157/month at 65+**.
  - **Private:**
    - Entry-age caps are common (typically 65–75). Sanitas and DKV accept up to 75. Adeslas Seniors runs 55–84. Asisa Completa Plus goes to 70 but has co-pays. DKV Famedic has no entry limit.
    - Example price: Adeslas Plena Plus in Andalusia, 2025 first-year rates, **€236/month at 65–69 and €245/month at 70+** per person.
  - https://www.sanidad.gob.es/en/servCiudadanos/internacional/convenioEspecial.htm ; https://www.thelocal.es/20250212/is-there-an-age-limit-for-spains-non-lucrative-visa ; https://guides.waypointsur.com/private-health-insurance-spain/
- **Special notes:**
  - **Modelo 720 (foreign-asset reporting) has not been replaced.** The tax agency still lists it as current, with a filing service for tax year 2025. Penalties now follow the general tax law after the EU court struck down the old regime in 2022. Crypto goes on the separate Modelo 721.
  - Residents with more than €1M of foreign financial assets also report to the Bank of Spain.
  - https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/procedimientoini/GI34.shtml ; https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/todas-gestiones/impuestos-tasas/declaraciones-informativas/modelo-720-decla_____sobre-bienes-derechos-extranjero_/preguntas-frecuentes/contribuyentes-obligados-declarar.html ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/spain/individual/other-issues

**BBD verdict:** Mixed, and it depends on the region. The worldwide wealth tax on the portfolio is the main drag, eased by netting margin debt and the 60%/80% cap. Dividends and interest pay 19–30%. Inheritance tax on US assets left to US-resident children appears avoidable. **Málaga (Andalusia)** is best below about €3.7M net wealth. **Valencia** is now good below €2M. **Tarragona (Catalonia)** is the worst, taxing from €500k.

**City notes:** Valencia → Valencian Community (€2M exemption from 2026; 99% inheritance relief). Málaga → Andalusia (no wealth tax up to about €3.7M, then solidarity-level rates; €1M plus 99% inheritance relief). Tarragona → Catalonia (€500k exemption, rates to 3.48%, sliding inheritance relief).

### France (Nice, Gascony/Gers)

- **Wealth tax:** the real-estate wealth tax (**IFI**) covers **real estate only**, so financial portfolios are outside it.
  - It applies when net real-estate wealth is above **€1.3M**. Rates run 0.5% (above €800k) to 1.5% (above €10M). Residents are taxed on worldwide real estate.
  - The "unproductive wealth tax" voted by the National Assembly in Oct 2025 (1% on a base including financial assets) was **dropped from the final 2026 budget** (adopted via Article 49.3, the government's power to pass a bill without a vote). The IFI stays as it was.
  - A new 2026 tax on luxury assets held in holding companies (a 20% rate per one commercial source) is not relevant to a directly held portfolio.
  - https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/france/individual/other-taxes ; https://2ndmarket.fr/actualites/plf-2026-ifi-improductif-fonds-euros-exit-tax-crypto
- **Inheritance tax:** if the deceased is a French tax resident, France taxes **worldwide assets**.
  - Each child gets a €100,000 allowance per parent. Rates then run 5% to €8,072, 10%, 15%, **20% from €15,933 to €552,324**, 30%, 40%, and **45% above €1,805,677**.
  - France also taxes an heir who has been French-resident for 6 of the last 10 years.
  - Life insurance (assurance-vie) has its own allowance (€152,500 per beneficiary for premiums paid before age 70, then 20%/31.25%), but carries US PFIC (passive foreign investment company) problems.
  - A **US–France estate and gift treaty exists**. It mainly allocates taxing rights by domicile and situs, with credits; it does not exempt. Its 7-of-10-years tie-breaker applies to people sent abroad by an employer (and their families), not retirees, so a retiree domiciled in France faces French tax on worldwide assets.
  - https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F14198 ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/france/individual/other-taxes ; https://www.taxesforexpats.com/country-guides/france/inheritance-tax-france.html ; https://sftaxcounsel.com/blog/a-closer-look-at-the-u-s-france-estate-and-gift-tax-treaty/ ; https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-gift-tax-treaties-international
- **Investment income tax:** residents are taxed on French and foreign investment income.
  - **2026 change:** the flat tax (PFU) is now **31.4%** (12.8% income tax plus **18.6%** social levies, up from 17.2%). It applies to dividends, interest and capital gains.
  - You can instead opt for the progressive scale, with a 40% dividend allowance.
  - The 3%/4% high-income surcharge (CEHR) and the 20% minimum tax on high incomes (CDHR, above €250k single / €500k couple) can add on top.
  - The 50% foreign-income exemption (art. 155 B) is only for people moving to France for work.
  - **Exit tax:** applies on leaving France after 6 of the last 10 years as a resident, with more than €800k of securities. Deferral is automatic for EU moves. The relief period is 2 years (5 above €2.57M). Reports that LF 2026 restored a 15-year period conflict and were not confirmed.
  - https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/france/individual/income-determination ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/france/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://www.taxesforexpats.com/country-guides/france/france-exit-tax.html
- **Residency route:** the **long-stay visitor visa** (VLS-TS "visiteur"), then a renewable 1-year visitor residence card.
  - It requires at least **€1,477.93 net/month (€17,735.19/year)** for a single person, health insurance for the whole stay, and a handwritten pledge not to work.
  - The 2026 budget raises the residence-card fee to €300 plus a €25 admin fee (stamp duty), and validation of the visa after arrival to €300.
  - https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F302?lang=en ; https://www.connexionfrance.com/news/residency-changes-in-france-more-americans-card-fees-set-to-rise-and-test-centre-update/770707
- **Healthcare:**
  - **Public:** inactive residents join **PUMa**, the universal public health cover, after **3 months'** stable, regular residence, and must live in France at least 6 months a year.
  - The 2026 social security financing law adds a new contribution for inactive PUMa members who pay no CSG/CRDS (social charges on income). Its amount awaits a decree.
  - **Annual "PUMa tax" (CSM):** **6.5%** of capital income above 50% of the social security ceiling (€24,030 for 2026). It applies when earned income is below €9,612, and the base is capped at €384,480. People with a pension are exempt; whether a US Social Security pension counts is **unverified**. This bites a BBD retiree with high dividends and no pension.
  - **Complementary insurance (mutuelle):** about **€120–132/month at age 70** (€110 at 60, €140 at 80). Some insurers refuse new members over 70. Full private expat cover is needed for the first 3 months and for the visa.
  - https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F34308?lang=en ; https://www.indy.fr/guide/fiscalite/gestion-financiere/simulateur-taxe-puma/ ; https://reassurez-moi.fr/guide/mutuelle-sante/prix-mutuelle-senior

**City notes:** the rules are national. Nice (Alpes-Maritimes) has high property costs, so IFI is more likely if you buy property worth more than €1.3M. In rural Gers, costs are low and IFI is unlikely, but specialists are sparser (qualitative, not sourced).

  - Residents are taxed on worldwide income.
  - Dividends: 5%, a final tax. Interest: 15%, a final tax. Gains on listed shares are exempt for holdings under 0.5%.
  - **Art. 5B (7% regime):** a 7% flat tax on *all* foreign-source income for up to 15 years. It requires foreign pension income, no Greek tax residence in 5 of the prior 6 years, and prior residence in a country with a tax cooperation agreement. It cannot be extended to family members. The IBA article does not explicitly name dividends and interest, so confirm they are covered.
  - **Art. 5A (non-dom):** €100k a year covers all foreign income and gains, plus €20k per family member, for up to 15 years. Conditions: no Greek tax residence in 7 of the prior 8 years, and a €500k Greek investment within 3 years (real estate, unlisted shares, Greek bonds, funds, or listed instruments).
  - Payment deadline: one secondary source says the 5A/5B payment date moved in 2026 (unverified).
  - Sources: https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/greece/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/greece/individual/income-determination ; https://www.ibanet.org/preferential-tax-regimes-for-individuals-moving-their-tax-residency-to-Greece
- **Residency routes:**
  - Financially Independent Person (FIP) permit, Law 5038/2023 art. 163(8): at least €3,500/month of passive income, +20% for a spouse and +15% per child. Valid 3 years and renewable. Renewal requires at least 183 days a year in Greece. No work allowed.
  - Third-party sources disagree on a savings alternative; I did not verify this against the Greek government site.
  - The Golden Visa (property) was not researched here.
  - Source: https://vardikos.com/what-is-financially-independent-persons-fip-visa
- **Healthcare:**
  - Private insurance is needed for the FIP application.
  - Holders of a "pensioner" residence permit (with a foreign pension, AMKA number and no other public cover) can buy into public EOPYY-type cover. Cost is 8.5% of monthly pension income. It covers hospital, primary care and diagnostics, with a 25% co-pay on medicines.
  - Private cost and entry-age caps (international plans):
    - Cigna Global states "no upper age limit" (subject to underwriting).
    - APRIL International's long-term plan takes new joiners only up to age 70.
    - Feather quotes from €559/month at age 65–69 in Spain/Portugal (a proxy; no Greece figure).
  - Local Greek insurer entry caps: not verified.
  - Sources: https://www.argophilia.com/news/optional-health-insurance-for-third-country-pensioners-terms-and-procedures/243223 ; https://www.cignaglobal.com/seniors-health-insurance ; https://www.april-international.com/en/long-term-international-health-insurance/long-term-international-health-insurance-plan ; https://feather-insurance.com/en-ww/blog/international-health-insurance-senior
- **BBD verdict:** Good. A 5% dividend tax, no wealth tax, mild inheritance tax for children (10% top rate), and an estate treaty with the US. The 7% regime needs a pension. The €100k lump sum makes sense only for foreign income above roughly €1.4M a year, but it also shields foreign assets from Greek inheritance tax.
- **City notes:** Athens (Attica) is excluded from the 2026 village ENFIA relief. Crete is the same national regime.

### Italy (Florence)

- **Wealth tax:**
  - **IVAFE:** 0.2% a year on the value of financial assets held abroad at 31 December (0.4% in "privileged" jurisdictions). A US brokerage account pays 0.2% of market value every year, which matters directly for BBD. Foreign bank accounts pay a flat €34.20 each.
  - **IVIE:** 1.06% on foreign real estate.
  - Italian-held securities pay a 0.2% stamp duty instead.
  - Sources: https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/italy/individual/other-taxes ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/quick-charts/net-wealth-worth-tax-rates
- **Inheritance/estate tax:**
  - Spouse and direct line: 4% above a €1M allowance *per heir*. Siblings: 6% above €100k. Others: 6–8%.
  - Self-assessed from 2025.
  - Scope over a resident decedent's worldwide assets: not stated on PwC (I believe it is worldwide; not verified).
  - A US–Italy estate tax treaty exists (1955).
  - Sources: https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/italy/individual/other-taxes ; IRS link above
- **Dividends, interest, foreign income:**
  - Foreign dividends: a 26% substitute tax. It applies to the gross amount when received without an Italian intermediary, and is self-assessed in Quadro RM.
  - Foreign tax credit: the Revenue Agency has historically denied it. Cassation rulings 25698/2022 and 10204/2024 say the treaty decides, and the US–Italy treaty art. 23 means the credit is due where the substitute tax is mandatory.
  - Capital gains on financial assets: 26%. Crypto rises to 33% from FY2026.
  - Foreign interest rate: not confirmed on a fetched page (26% expected).
  - **Flat tax for new residents (art. 24-bis):** **€300,000 a year** for those moving from 1 January 2026 (€200k for 2025 movers, €100k for 2024), plus €50k per family member. It replaces income tax on foreign income *and* IVAFE/IVIE. It says nothing about inheritance tax.
  - **7% regime:** applies to foreign pensions and other foreign income for 10 years. It requires foreign pension income, a move to a municipality under 20,000 inhabitants in Sicily, Calabria, Sardinia, Campania, Basilicata, Abruzzo, Molise or Puglia, and 5 years of prior non-residence. **Florence (Tuscany) does not qualify.**
  - Sources: https://www.we-wealth.com/en?p=131082 ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/italy/individual/income-determination ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/italy/individual/taxes-on-personal-income
- **Residency routes:**
  - Elective Residence visa. The Houston consulate requires at least €31,000 a year for one person and €62,000 for a married couple, from pensions, annuities, property or similar passive sources. It also requires a registered lease or deed covering the whole visa period. No work allowed.
  - A law firm notes the figure is consular practice rather than statute. The permit runs 1 year and is renewable, and absences over 6 consecutive months put renewal at risk.
  - Sources: https://conshouston.esteri.it/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/ELECTIVE-RESIDENCE.docx ; https://www.boccadutri.com/elective-residence-permit-italy-requirements/
- **Healthcare:**
  - Elective residents can join the public SSN by voluntary registration. The fee is 7.5% of income up to €20,658 plus 4% up to €51,646, with a €2,000 minimum (raised by the 2024 budget law) and a €2,788.87 maximum per year. Figures are for 2025 (Milan).
  - The visa itself requires private cover of at least €30k.
  - Private/international plans: see the Greece notes.
  - Sources: https://www.thelocal.it/20250130/how-much-does-it-cost-to-register-with-italys-national-health-service-in-2025 ; https://www.boccadutri.com/elective-residence-permit-italy-requirements/
- **BBD verdict:** Poor under ordinary rules. Italy taxes dividends at 26% (stacked on US tax, with the credit contested) and charges a 0.2% IVAFE on the whole US portfolio every year. The €300k flat tax removes both, but pays off only for very large portfolios (roughly €1M+ of foreign income).
- **City notes:** Florence is outside the 7% southern regime. The SSN fee is capped at about €2.8k a year, which makes public healthcare cheap.

### Croatia (Split)

- **Wealth tax:** None. — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/croatia/individual/other-taxes ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/quick-charts/net-wealth-worth-tax-rates
- **Inheritance/estate tax:**
  - The rate is 4%, but **spouse and descendants are exempt**.
  - The Tax Authority applies a territorial principle: an inheritance located abroad, such as funds in foreign bank accounts, is not taxed in Croatia.
  - No US estate treaty.
  - Sources: https://porezna-uprava.gov.hr/en/inheritanc-e-and-gifts-tax-information-on-the-general-rules-rates-and-taxpayer/7315 ; https://eurofast.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Croatia-Tax-Card-2026.pdf ; https://www.forvismazars.com/hr/en/insights/forvis-mazars-tax-newsletter/mazars-tax-news-october-2022
- **Dividends, interest, foreign income:**
  - Worldwide taxation. Interest, dividends and capital gains: **12%** final tax. Gains on financial assets are taxable if sold within 2 years; the treatment after 2 years is implied exempt but not confirmed on the fetched page.
  - Pensions and other regular income are taxed at local progressive rates: 15–22% / 25–32% for cities such as Split, and 17–23.6% / 27–35.4% under the 2026 Tax Card bands.
  - No special regime for incoming retirees.
  - No US–Croatia income tax treaty is in force.
  - Sources: https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/croatia/individual/income-determination ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/croatia/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://www.taxesforexpats.com/country-guides/hr/retire-in-croatia.html
- **Residency routes (weak):**
  - The Interior Ministry lists the grounds for temporary stay as: family, study, research, humanitarian, partnership, work, posted work, long-term EU residents, "other purposes" and digital nomads.
  - **No retirement or property ground is listed.** "Other purposes" and nomad stays can only be reapplied for 6 months after the previous one expires.
  - The digital nomad permit requires remote *work* (not passive income) and €3,622.50/month or a €43,470 lump sum. It lasts at most 18 months.
  - One secondary source claims property ownership eases renewal for over-60s. This contradicts the ministry list and is unverified.
  - Sources: https://mup.gov.hr/aliens-281621/stay-and-work/temporary-stay-of-third-country-nationals/281661 ; https://mup.gov.hr/aliens-281621/stay-and-work/temporary-stay-of-digital-nomads/286833 ; https://www.taxesforexpats.com/country-guides/hr/retire-in-croatia.html
- **Healthcare:**
  - Non-EU residents need health insurance to apply and then register with HZZO (state insurance), according to the taxesforexpats page.
  - The monthly contribution for a non-EU retiree is **not verified**: the only figures found are about €70–110/month from a student-insurance site, which I did not fetch.
  - Private plans: see the Greece notes.
  - Source: https://www.taxesforexpats.com/country-guides/hr/retire-in-croatia.html
- **BBD verdict:** Taxes are excellent: 12% on dividends, no wealth tax, and no inheritance tax for children on foreign assets. But there is no reliable long-term residence route for a non-EU retiree with only passive income.
- **City notes:** Split sets its own income tax rates within the city bands. These matter for pensions, not for the flat 12% on capital income.

### Montenegro (Kotor)

- **Inheritance/estate tax:**

### Cyprus (Paphos / Limassol)

- **Wealth tax:** None. Immovable property tax was abolished in 2017. — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/cyprus/individual/other-taxes ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/quick-charts/net-wealth-worth-tax-rates
- **Inheritance/estate tax:** None. There is no gift tax on gifts to relatives up to the third degree. No US estate treaty (none needed). — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/quick-charts/inheritance-and-gift-tax-rates
- **Dividends, interest, foreign income:**
  - Dividends and interest are exempt from income tax. The Special Defence Contribution (SDC) applies only to residents who are also Cyprus-domiciled.
  - **Non-doms (domicile of origin outside Cyprus) are exempt from SDC until they have been tax resident 17 of the last 20 years**, so dividends and interest are effectively tax-free.
  - The 2026 reform cut SDC on dividends for domiciled residents from 17% to 5%; interest stays at 17%.
  - After the 17 years, an optional **€250,000 lump sum covers 5 years** of SDC (art. 3D). Whether it can be extended was not confirmed on the fetched page.
  - The General Healthcare System (GHS/GeSY) contribution of **2.65% applies to dividend and interest income, capped at €180k of income**, so at most about €4.8k a year. PwC does not carve out non-doms (likely applies; confirm).
  - No tax on capital gains on shares. Foreign pensions are taxed at 5% above €5,000.
  - The 60-day residence rule was not verified here.
  - Sources: https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/cyprus/individual/income-determination ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/cyprus/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/cyprus/individual/other-taxes ; https://cy.andersen.com/news/cyprus-introduces-an-alternative-sdc-regime/
- **Residency routes:**
  - **Category F** permanent residence: secured income from abroad of at least €9,568 a year, +€4,613 per dependant. Dividends and interest qualify. No property purchase is needed; a rental is acceptable. No work allowed. The permit lapses after 2 years' absence. Medical tests are required from 1 September 2026. No official processing time.
  - **PR by investment:** new residential property of at least €300k + VAT, plus €50k/year of foreign income (+€15k spouse, +€10k per child). Processing about 2–4 months.
  - Sources: https://gk-lawfirm.com/practice-areas/immigration-law/cyprus-permanent-residency/category-f-visa ; https://cyprus-mail.com/2026/01/28/cyprus-residency-requirements-visas-permits-and-permanent-residence-explained
- **Healthcare:**
  - Holders of Category F or Regulation 6(2) permits can register in GeSY if habitually resident; dependants follow.
  - Private plans: see the Greece notes.
  - Source: https://www.demetriadeslaw.com/what-is-gesy/
- **BBD verdict:** Best fit. Dividends and interest are effectively taxed at only the 2.65% GeSY contribution (capped) for 17 years. There is no wealth or inheritance tax, no capital gains tax on shares, and permanent residence is cheap (Category F). The remaining tax burden is the US one.
- **City notes:** Limassol is pricier and more business-oriented. Paphos is the more retiree-oriented of the two. Both are under the same national regime.

### Malta (Valletta / Sliema)

- **Wealth tax:** None. — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/malta/individual/other-taxes
- **Inheritance/estate tax:**
  - No inheritance, estate or gift tax. Stamp duty applies on inheriting *Maltese* property (5%) or Maltese shares (2%).
  - A US portfolio would not be dutiable.
  - Sources: https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/malta/individual/other-taxes ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/quick-charts/inheritance-and-gift-tax-rates
- **Dividends, interest, foreign income:**
  - **Remittance basis for resident non-doms:** foreign income is taxed only if remitted to Malta, and foreign capital gains are not taxed even if remitted.
  - A €5,000 minimum tax applies if foreign income is at least €35k.
  - **Global Residence Programme (GRP):** 15% on remitted foreign income, €15k minimum. It requires a property of at least €275k (purchase) or €9,600/year (rent).
  - **The GRP closes to new applicants after 31 December 2026.** The Individual Tax Programme Rules 2026 (LN 195/2026) apply from 1 January 2027:
    - Global Resident status: €35k minimum tax.
    - Retired Pensioner status: €15k minimum tax.
    - Property: at least €700k purchase or €14k/year rent.
    - Rate: still 15% on remitted income.
  - **GRP applications filed by 31 December 2026 keep the old terms until 31 December 2031.**
  - Malta Retirement Programme (MRP): 15%, €7,500 minimum. Pension must be at least 75% of chargeable income.
  - Sources: https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/malta/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://www.deloitte.com/mt/en/services/tax/perspectives/tax-alerts/2026/individual-tax-programme-rules-2026.html ; https://pkfmalta.com/news/network-news/malta-s-2027-individual-tax-programme-new-thresholds-and-new-planning-priorities/
- **Residency routes:**
  - GRP / ITP status, as above (tax status plus a residence permit route for third-country nationals).
  - **MPRP** (permanent residence):
    - Capital of €500k (at least €150k liquid) or €650k (at least €75k liquid).
    - Property: buy at least €375k, or rent at least €14k/year.
    - Fees: €60k administration, €37k government contribution, €2k NGO donation (€99k in fees).
    - Health insurance must be kept for 5 years.
  - Sources: https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/malta/immigration/malta-permanent-residence-programme-%28mprp%29-2026-requirements-costs-timeline ; https://pkfmalta.com/news/network-news/malta-s-2027-individual-tax-programme-new-thresholds-and-new-planning-priorities/
- **Healthcare:**
  - Qualifying private cover is required for all non-EU residents (MPRP, GRP and others). Free public care is tied to social security contributions.
  - Private plans: see the Greece notes.
  - Source: https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/malta/food-drugs-healthcare-life-sciences/healthcare-system-in-malta-a-detailed-overview
- **BBD verdict:** Very good if you leave dividends un-remitted in the US account. That costs only about €5k of minimum tax a year (more under GRP/ITP), with no wealth or inheritance tax. But it is unverified whether bringing margin-loan proceeds into Malta counts as a remittance, and the cheap GRP terms close on 31 December 2026.
- **City notes:** Sliema/Valletta are in the full-price property tier. Lower GRP thresholds apply in the south of Malta and in Gozo (until 2027).

### Panama (Boquete, Panama City)

- **1. Wealth tax:** None. PwC: "There are no net wealth/worth taxes in Panama." The annual property tax on real estate still applies. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/panama/individual/other-taxes
- **2. Inheritance/estate tax:** None. PwC: "There are no inheritance, estate, or gift taxes in Panama." No US estate tax treaty exists. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/panama/individual/other-taxes ; https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-gift-tax-treaties-international
- **3. Dividends/interest/foreign income:** The system is territorial. PwC: "Panama's tax system is based on a territorial concept of income." Foreign-source dividends and interest from a US brokerage are therefore outside Panamanian tax. PwC states the territorial principle but does not mention foreign dividends or interest by name; that conclusion follows from the principle. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/panama/individual/taxes-on-personal-income
- **4. Residency on passive income or assets:**
  - **Pensionado:** requires a *lifetime pension* of at least US$1,000/month, plus US$250 per dependent. Investment income does not qualify.
  - **Friendly Nations:** requires an economic tie to Panama: a local job, Panamanian real estate of US$200k or more, or a Panamanian fixed-term deposit of US$200k or more. It starts as 2 years of provisional residency, then permanent.
  - **Qualified Investor:** permanent residency, sometimes in about 30 days, for one of: US$300k or more in Panamanian real estate, US$500k or more in approved securities (local), or US$750k or more in a fixed-term deposit.
  - **Economic Solvency:** a US$300k Panamanian time deposit held for 3 years, or real estate.
  - A US brokerage portfolio does not qualify by itself; capital has to be placed in Panama.
  - Whether US citizens are on the Friendly Nations list was **not verified** in a fetched source.
  - https://www.residepanama.com/en/reside-panama-blog/panama-visa-options-for-expats-in-2026-the-complete-guide
- **5. Healthcare:**
  - The CSS public system is tied to payroll contributions. The fetched source does not confirm that retirees can join.
  - Private local cover for an expat in their 60s and older costs about US$350–700+/month. Entry-age caps of 65–70 for local insurers are reported, but not verified in a fetched source.
  - Panama City has strong private hospitals: Hospital Punta Pacífica (Johns Hopkins Medicine affiliate), Pacífica Salud, Hospital Nacional and Paitilla.
  - Boquete has little locally. Residents use private hospitals in David (Hospital Chiriquí, Mae Lewis) and fly to Panama City, about 1 hour, for specialist care.
  - https://www.residepanama.com/en/reside-panama-blog/healthcare-in-panama-an-expat-guide-to-hospitals-insurance-costs-2026
- **BBD verdict:** Among the best fits: no tax on US dividends or interest, no wealth tax and no inheritance tax. The catch is residency, which needs a pension or capital parked in Panama (US$200k to US$300k or more).
- **City notes:** Panama City has top-tier private care. Boquete depends on David or Panama City for anything serious.

  - Residents in these categories must join CAJA (CCSS). In 2022 the premium was reported at US$200–400/month for US$1,500–2,500 declared income. A 2022 bill to make enrolment voluntary was filed; whether it passed was not verified. https://qcostarica.com/foreign-investors-pensioners-and-rentiers-not-be-required-to-pay-the-caja/
  - Private care in San José: Hospital Clínica Bíblica is JCI-accredited and the only Costa Rican hospital on the Newsweek/Statista "Latin America's Top Private Hospitals & Clinics 2026" list. https://ticotimes.net/2026/02/06/costa-rican-hospital-climbs-ranks-among-latin-americas-best-in-2026
  - CIMA's JCI status was not verified in a fetched source.
- **BBD verdict:** Excellent today (dividends untaxed, no wealth or inheritance tax), but Bill 25.796 could add a 15% tax on foreign dividends and interest. Watch it before committing.
- **City notes:** San José and Escazú have the country's best private hospitals.

### Mexico (Puerto Vallarta, Mérida, San Miguel de Allende)

- **1. Wealth tax:** None. PwC: "There are no net wealth/worth taxes in Mexico." https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/mexico/individual/other-taxes
- **2. Inheritance/estate tax:** No specific tax. "Income received by a Mexican resident through inheritance is exempt from income tax." US heirs who are not resident in Mexico, inheriting US assets, should fall outside Mexican tax (my inference). No US treaty exists. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/mexico/individual/other-taxes
- **3. Dividends/interest/foreign income:** Mexico taxes worldwide income. "Resident individuals are subject to Mexican income tax on their worldwide income". The 2026 top rate is 35%.
  - Foreign dividends are "fully taxable in the annual tax return," *plus* a separate 10% tax on the net dividend that "cannot be credited in the return."
  - Foreign interest is fully taxable, including FX gains on principal.
  - Relief for US withholding under the US–Mexico income tax treaty and foreign tax credit is likely but not detailed on the fetched page.
  - https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/mexico/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/mexico/individual/income-determination
- **4. Residency on passive income or assets:** A temporary resident visa on economic solvency accepts *savings or investment* balances. The San Diego consulate's 2026 sheet requires either:
  - a minimum monthly balance of **US$68,241** for the previous 12 months, or
  - income of **US$4,049/month** for 6 months.

  Figures vary by consulate. A brokerage account qualifies as "investments." https://consulmex.sre.gob.mx/sandiego/images/visas/ingles/5a_Temporary_Resident_Visa_-_Economic_Solvency.pdf
- **5. Healthcare:**
  - IMSS voluntary "Seguro de Salud para la Familia" fees from 1 Mar 2026 are MXN 20,600/yr at age 60–69, 21,500 at 70–79 and 22,150 at 80+, paid annually in advance. Pre-existing-condition exclusions and waiting periods were not verified. https://imss.gob.mx/faq/seguro-familia
  - Private local insurance example: about MXN 96,000/yr for a healthy 62-year-old, with a high deductible and annual increases. Medicare is generally not accepted. https://www.sanmiguelscene.com/blog/long-term-care-in-san-miguel-de-allende
- **BBD verdict:** Poor for a dividend-heavy portfolio. Dividends are taxed at up to 35% plus a 10% levy that cannot be credited. Residency is easy and healthcare is cheap, but a growth/low-yield tilt is essential.
- **City notes:**
  - San Miguel de Allende has two private hospitals (e.g. MAC). Larger centres are in Querétaro (about 1 hr) and Mexico City (about 3 hr). https://www.sanmiguelscene.com/blog/long-term-care-in-san-miguel-de-allende
  - Mérida is a regional referral hub; it is where Belize sends serious cases. https://www.riotimesonline.com/healthcare-belize-expats-2026/
  - Puerto Vallarta hospital quality was not verified.

### Colombia (Medellín)

- **1. Wealth tax:** Yes, on **worldwide** net wealth for residents ("bienes en el exterior (para residentes)").
  - Threshold: 72,000 UVT = **COP 3,770,928,000** (2026), measured at 1 January. At about COP 3,219/US$ (July 2026), that is roughly US$1.17M.
  - Marginal rates: 0.5% (72k–122k UVT), 1.0% (122k–239k UVT), **1.5%** above 239k UVT. The 1.5% band applies only for 2023–2026; it becomes 1% from 2027.
  - The home exclusion is up to 12,000 UVT.
  - The 2026 emergency decree that would have cut the threshold to 40,000 UVT (Decreto 1474/2025) was declared unconstitutional on 15 Apr 2026 (C-079/2026), so 72,000 UVT applies.
  - https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/colombia/individual/other-taxes ; https://accounter.co/noticias/editorial/abc-impuesto-al-patrimonio-2025 ; https://normograma.dian.gov.co/dian/compilacion/docs/oficio_dian_8914_2026.htm ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/quick-charts/net-wealth-worth-tax-rates ; https://colombiamove.com/blog/colombia-rentista-visa-v-rentista-guide/
- **2. Inheritance/estate tax:** Inheritances are "extraordinary income," taxed to the beneficiary as capital (occasional) gains at **15%**. How this applies when the heirs are non-resident and the assets are in the US was not verified. No US treaty exists. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/colombia/individual/other-taxes ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/quick-charts/inheritance-and-gift-tax-rates
- **3. Dividends/interest/foreign income:** Colombia taxes worldwide income. "Fiscal residents ... are taxed on worldwide income and should report equity owned in Colombia and abroad."
  - Progressive rates run 0–39%; the top rate applies above 31,000 UVT.
  - Interest falls in the general basket.
  - PwC does not set out the treatment of foreign dividends separately.
  - https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/colombia/individual/taxes-on-personal-income
- **4. Residency on passive income or assets:**
  - **V-Rentista:** 10 × SMMLV = **COP 17,509,050/month** (about US$5,440) of passive income. "Stock dividends and investment portfolio yields" qualify. It is a visitor (V) visa valid up to 2 years and does **not** count toward permanent residency. https://colombiamove.com/blog/colombia-rentista-visa-v-rentista-guide/
  - **M-Investor:** 350 SMMLV in real estate (about COP 578M, ~US$145k) or 650 SMMLV in a Colombian company, registered with Banco de la República. After 5 years it leads to the R visa. Portfolio investment likely does not qualify. https://www.globalinvestments.net/citizenship/colombia-investor-pensionado-visa
- **5. Healthcare:**
  - Rules on joining the EPS (public contributory system) depend on visa type. Sources conflict on whether M-Pensionado holders can join. Private "medicina prepagada" plans have product-specific entry-age limits (one SURA plan requires entry under 70; another states no maximum).
  - https://colombiamove.com/blog/eps-vs-medicina-prepagada-colombia-healthcare-guide/
  - Medellín hospitals in Newsweek's 2023 Colombia top 25 (2023 edition, not 2026): Pablo Tobón Uribe (#3), San Vicente Fundación (#4), Clínica Medellín (#11), Hospital General (#12), Las Américas (#18). https://www.elespectador.com/salud/estos-son-los-25-mejores-hospitales-de-colombia-segun-newsweek/
- **BBD verdict:** Bad for large estates: a worldwide wealth tax (up to 1.5%, 1% from 2027) above about US$1.2M, worldwide taxation of dividends up to 39%, and a 15% tax on inheritances. Visit, don't become tax resident (183 days).
- **City notes:** Medellín has excellent hospitals.

### Ecuador (Cuenca)

- **1. Wealth tax:** No general net wealth tax. The PwC "0.15" entry is the municipal 1.5‰ asset tax, which applies only to individuals required to keep accounting records. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/ecuador/individual/other-taxes ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/quick-charts/net-wealth-worth-tax-rates
- **2. Inheritance/estate tax:** Yes, and steep.
  - 2026 table (SRI Res. NAC-DGERCGC25-00000043): 0% to US$78,527, then rising in 5-point steps to **35% above US$942,353**.
  - Assets abroad of Ecuadorian residents are also subject to the tax.
  - No US treaty exists, so this tax could stack with US estate tax.
  - https://nmslaw.com.ec/blog/2026/01/05/tablas-impuesto-renta-2026-ecuador/ ; https://www.ecuadorlegalonline.com/sri/impuesto-herencia-ecuador/ ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/quick-charts/inheritance-and-gift-tax-rates
- **3. Dividends/interest/foreign income:** Ecuador taxes worldwide income. "Income generated abroad by Ecuador-resident individuals ... is taxable." A foreign tax credit applies up to the Ecuadorian tax. The 2026 top rate is 37% above US$109,956. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/ecuador/individual/taxes-on-personal-income
- **4. Residency on passive income or assets:**
  - **Rentista:** monthly income of at least 3 × SBU from rents abroad or in Ecuador ("arrendamiento, títulos de inversión y otros similares"), plus US$250 per dependent. Health insurance covering Ecuador is required. About US$1,446/month at SBU US$482; that SBU figure is from secondary sources.
  - **Investor:** 100 × SBU (about US$48,200), not verified on the official page.
  - https://www.gob.ec/mremh/tramites/concesion-visa-residencia-temporal-rentista
- **5. Healthcare:**
  - Foreign residents with a cédula can join the IESS voluntarily at 17.60% of declared income; the 2026 minimum is US$84.86/month. Pre-existing-condition rules and age limits were not stated. https://www.ecuadorlegalonline.com/laboral/afiliacion-voluntaria-iess/
  - Cuenca hospitals: Hospital Universitario del Río, Santa Inés and Monte Sinaí. Consultations cost US$20–50. https://cuencahighlife.com/navigating-healthcare-in-cuenca-a-comprehensive-guide-for-us-expats/
- **BBD verdict:** Poor: worldwide tax on dividends (up to 37%) and an inheritance tax of up to 35% that reaches foreign assets. Cheap, decent care and easy residency don't offset this for a large estate.
- **City notes:** Cuenca has good, inexpensive private hospitals.

### Uruguay (Montevideo)

- **1. Wealth tax:** Yes, but only on net wealth **located in Uruguay**. Residents pay 0.1% (2026) above about US$160k of local net wealth, doubled for family units. A US brokerage account is outside the base. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/uruguay/individual/other-taxes ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/quick-charts/net-wealth-worth-tax-rates
- **2. Inheritance/estate tax:** None ("NA" in PwC). No US treaty exists. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/quick-charts/inheritance-and-gift-tax-rates
- **3. Dividends/interest/foreign income:**
  - Income is mostly source-based, but residents pay IRPF at **12%** on foreign passive income (interest and dividends), levied on gross income. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/uruguay/individual/taxes-on-personal-income
  - **Ley 20.446** (2025–29 budget, effective 1 Jan 2026):
    - New residents can elect a full exemption on foreign capital income for the arrival year plus 10 years (**11 in total**), then 5 years at **6%**.
    - Qualifying routes: more than 183 days of presence, or about US$2M of real estate, or US$100k/yr into the Innovation Fund.
    - Foreign capital gains are now taxed at 12% for those not on the holiday.
    - The old permanent **7% option** is reported as being phased out for new residents.
    - https://www.imidaily.com/latin-america/uruguay-raises-tax-holiday-threshold-to-us2-million-taxes-foreign-income-at-12/
  - These details come from a trade-news source. The law text and DGI regulation were not read, and secondary sources conflict on 6% versus 7%.
- **4. Residency on passive income or assets:** Legal residency for a "rentista ... en el exterior" requires a notarial certificate of the nature and monthly amount of the income. **No fixed minimum is published.** https://www.gub.uy/tramites/residencia-legal-permanente
- **5. Healthcare:**
  - The public provider ASSE serves residents without FONASA coverage; those above an income threshold pay a Cuota ASSE.
  - Self-pay mutualista membership is about US$90–140/adult/month (one 2026 source), with age-based admission rules and senior surcharges.
  - The main providers in Montevideo are Hospital Británico, Asociación Española, Médica Uruguaya, CASMU and others.
  - https://www.riotimesonline.com/healthcare-uruguay-expats-2026/
  - Reported age caps for new members (Británico refusing over 60–65; Médica Uruguaya accepting 70s) were *not verified* in a fetched source.
- **BBD verdict:** Very strong for the first 11 years: 0% on foreign dividends and interest, no inheritance tax, and wealth tax only on local assets. After that the rate is 6%, then 12%. Best long-horizon "worldwide but light" option.
- **City notes:** Montevideo holds most of the country's advanced specialty care.

### Belize (Cayo/San Ignacio, Ambergris Caye)

- **1. Wealth tax:** No source fetched. PwC does not cover Belize. I believe there is none, but this is **unverified**.
- **2. Inheritance/estate tax:** The Belize Tourism Board lists QRP members as exempt from inheritance tax (and capital gains tax). No US treaty exists. https://www.belizetourismboard.org/programs-events/retirement-program/ ; https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-gift-tax-treaties-international
- **3. Dividends/interest/foreign income:** QRP members "shall be exempted from all taxes and duties on income received from a source outside of Belize." Belize's general rules for non-QRP residents were **not verified**. https://www.belizetourismboard.org/programs-events/retirement-program/
- **4. Residency on passive income or assets:** The QRP requires:
  - age **40+**;
  - **US$2,000/month** (US$24k/yr) of income from outside Belize, with a written undertaking to deposit it in a Belize institution;
  - at least 30 consecutive days a year in Belize.

  Accepted sources explicitly include "Investment Account," personal savings and inheritance, so **investment income qualifies**. https://www.belizetourismboard.org/programs-events/retirement-program/
- **5. Healthcare:**
  - Weak. The US Embassy says care "does not meet United States standards."
  - Ambergris Caye has only clinics (San Pedro Polyclinic) and no hospital.
  - Cayo has a community hospital in San Ignacio, the private La Loma Luz in Santa Elena and a regional hospital in Belmopan.
  - Serious cases go to Karl Heusner (Belize City) first, then by air to **Mérida, Guatemala City, Cancún or San Pedro Sula**, or to Miami.
  - Medevac "can easily cost US$10,000 and up."
  - https://www.riotimesonline.com/healthcare-belize-expats-2026/
- **BBD verdict:** QRP is tax-perfect for BBD (all foreign income exempt, investment accounts accepted, only US$2k/mo). Healthcare is the dealbreaker: budget for international cover with evacuation.
- **City notes:** Cayo is closer to Guatemala and Belmopan. Ambergris Caye means a boat or flight for anything beyond a clinic.

### Placencia (Belize)

Researched 2026-11-06. Country facts (QRP, tax, inheritance, healthcare in general) are in the Belize section above; this covers what differs at city level.

- **Tax, residency, estate:** same as Belize above (QRP exempts all foreign-source income; investment accounts qualify; QRP members exempt from inheritance tax). Nothing city-level changes the BBD tax profile.
- **Healthcare:**
  - **Placencia Polyclinic** (public; Ministry of Health and Wellness with the Belize Tourism Board) opened 15 May 2024 near the airstrip: consultation and triage rooms, pharmacy, laboratory, shock/trauma rooms and a donated ambulance. It serves Placencia Village, Seine Bight and nearby communities. https://www.belizetourismboard.org/?p=39983
  - The Health Minister said it would run 24 hours, but in October 2024 a parent who arrived with a feverish child in the early hours found it **deserted**, with no staff or security. No official reply was reported. https://www.greaterbelize.com/is-the-placencia-polyclinic-truly-open-24-7/
  - **Nearest hospital: yes, Southern Regional Hospital, Dangriga** (public; general, maternity, intensive and surgical care). It is the only Stann Creek facility on the UK's medical list (last updated June 2021). https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/belize-list-of-medical-facilities/belize-medical-facilities
  - Dangriga is **about 1¼–1¾ h by road**: shuttle 1 h 15 min, bus 1 h 21 min (https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Placencia/Dangriga); 1 h 45 min (https://www.belizehub.com/?p=26828). Road distance was not read from a source; the 21-mile peninsula road (Moon, below) plus the Southern Highway leg suggests roughly 80 km (*my estimate*).
  - **Private care** is in Belize City: Belize Medical Associates and Belize Health Care Partners (24-hour emergency, CT) and Buttonwood Bay Medical & Cardiology. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/belize-list-of-medical-facilities/belize-medical-facilities That is about 3½ h by road (https://www.belizehub.com/?p=26828), or a daytime flight from the airstrip (Access, below).
  - **Evacuation:** Tropic Air runs medical transfers 24 h a day only from San Pedro and Caye Caulker to Belize City; its regional transfers go to Mérida, Cancún, San Pedro Sula and Guatemala City. Placencia is **not** listed as an origin, and passengers must be stable and need no in-flight care. https://www.tropicair.com/medical-transfer/ Wings of Hope Belize, a domestic air ambulance, flies from Punta Gorda, Dangriga, the cayes and other districts to Belize City; Placencia is not named. https://rainforestrealty.com/?p=29084 Serious cases otherwise follow the national route: Belize City, then Mérida, Guatemala City or Miami (Belize section above).
  - Net: worse than Cayo (which has a community hospital, a private hospital in Santa Elena and Belmopan's regional hospital about 30 km away), and comparable to Ambergris Caye (a clinic plus a boat or flight), with a longer road leg to private care.
- **Safety:**
  - US: **Level 2** (Exercise Increased Caution) for crime, updated 12 March 2026; Southside Belize City is **Level 3**, and the advisory warns against driving between cities after dark. Stann Creek and Placencia are not singled out. (Read via the report https://www.greaterbelize.com/?p=86159 ; travel.state.gov returned HTTP 403.)
  - UK: "Belize has one of the highest per capita murder rates in the world"; gang violence is concentrated in Belize City's Southside. Placencia is named with San Pedro, Caye Caulker and San Ignacio as a place where tourists are "vulnerable to theft". It warns of flooded temporary bridges and causeways in severe weather, "particularly in Stann Creek and Toledo". https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/belize/safety-and-security
- **Retiree community:**
  - Small. The village had 1,512 people (2010 census), the peninsula 3,458 including expats; a 2024 estimate puts the village under 2,000. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placencia No expat headcount was found; promotional pages say "thousands of retirees and expatriates" have discovered it, with no count. https://www.belizehub.com/?p=18565
  - Areas: Placencia Village at the tip (the hub, one main street); **Maya Beach**, a small community midway up the peninsula with homes, small resorts, two small grocery stores and restaurants; and **Seine Bight**, a historic Garifuna village next to Maya Beach. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placencia ; "a healthy number of expats", with resorts in Seine Bight and Maya Beach north of the village. https://www.afar.com/places/placencia-peninsula-stann-creek-district
  - English: Belize is the only Central American country with English as the official language. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize
- **Access:**
  - Placencia airstrip (PLJ): a 650 m asphalt runway, served by Maya Island Air and Tropic Air to Belize City (international and municipal), Dangriga and Punta Gorda. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placencia_Airport Moon counts **more than 20 daily flights** at the airstrip; planes usually run Belize City → Dangriga → Placencia → Punta Gorda. https://www.moontravelguides.com/travel/planning/getting-to-and-around-belizes-placencia-village/ Tropic Air flies "multiple times a day"; flight time was not stated on the pages read. https://www.tropicair.com/destinations/placencia/
  - Road to BZE: **about 3½ h** (https://www.belizehub.com/?p=26828); "three or four hours" from Belize City via the Hummingbird and Southern Highways and the 21-mile peninsula road, paved in 2010 (Moon, above).
  - US nonstops from BZE: year-round Atlanta (Delta), Dallas/Fort Worth and Miami (American), Houston IAH (United) and Hobby (Southwest), Los Angeles (Alaska); seasonal Charlotte, Chicago, Denver, Newark, San Francisco, Minneapolis, Seattle, Baltimore, plus BermudAir's new seasonal routes from December 2026. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_S._W._Goldson_International_Airport
- **Climate:** tropical monsoon. Highs 27 °C (Dec–Jan) to 32 °C (Apr–Sep), lows 20–24 °C. Rain is heaviest June–October (290–350 mm a month; July wettest), driest Feb–Apr (66–82 mm). https://weather2travel.com/belize/placencia/climate Hurricane season is 1 June–30 November (UK, above). **Hurricane Iris** (Cat 4, 8 October 2001) made landfall at Monkey River Town just south of the peninsula and damaged nearly 95% of Placencia's buildings. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placencia ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize
- **Costs:** Numbeo's Placencia page was rate-limited (HTTP 429) and livingcost.org has no Placencia page, so indices are mapped between the Cayo and Ambergris Caye rows (docs/research/cost-check-2026-11.md, Placencia).
- **BBD-relevant differences from Cayo:**
  - Same QRP tax treatment, so the tax and estate case is identical.
  - Hospital access is the binding difference: a polyclinic of uncertain night staffing, 1¼–1¾ h to a public hospital and 3½ h by road to private care, versus Cayo's local hospitals.
  - Hurricane exposure on a low coastal peninsula (Cayo is inland); property insurance and evacuation logistics cost more.
  - Costs are higher (beach rents and restaurants), but better daily air links to BZE than Cayo has by road.
- **Top 50?** Not on the current evidence. It has the same profile as Ambergris Caye (excluded: no hospital, everything beyond a clinic means a trip out), plus a longer road leg and hurricane exposure. Cayo carries Belize in the 50.

### Dominican Republic (Las Terrenas)

- **1. Wealth tax:** None. PwC: "There are no net wealth/worth taxes in the Dominican Republic." https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/dominican-republic/individual/other-taxes
- **2. Inheritance/estate tax:** **3%** inheritance tax on successors; gifts carry 27%. Whether it reaches foreign assets of a resident decedent is not stated, so worldwide reach is **unverified**. No US treaty exists. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/dominican-republic/individual/other-taxes
- **3. Dividends/interest/foreign income:**
  - The system is territorial, but residents are taxed on "foreign investments and financial gains." New residents are taxed on such foreign income "only after the third year."
  - The top rate is 25% in FY2026, rising to 27% in FY2027.
  - Law 171-07 says the income declared by pensionado and rentista beneficiaries is "exentas del Impuesto sobre la Renta." Whether this covers *all* foreign dividends beyond the qualifying amount is unclear.
  - https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/dominican-republic/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://phlaw.com/pensionado-o-rentista-bajo-la-ley-171-07/
- **4. Residency on passive income or assets:**
  - **Rentista (Law 171-07):** US$2,000/month of stable foreign income. Qualifying sources include investments in foreign banks or companies and interest from securities.
  - **Pensionado:** US$1,500/month plus US$250 per dependent.
  - **Investor:** about US$200k, from secondary sources only.
  - https://phlaw.com/pensionado-o-rentista-bajo-la-ley-171-07/
- **5. Healthcare:**
  - Las Terrenas has a public municipal hospital and two private clinics (Clínica Especializada Internacional; Centro Galeno Integral, offering cardiology and oncology). "Complex cases can be transferred to Santo Domingo or Santiago," 2–3 hours by road or by helicopter. https://playamax.com/?p=6331
  - Public access for foreigners and local ARS insurer age caps were **not verified**.
- **BBD verdict:** Mixed. Law 171-07 exemptions and the 3-year grace period help, but the territorial rule has a carve-out for foreign financial income and there is a 3% inheritance tax. Take local advice on whether dividends above the US$2k qualifying amount are exempt.
- **City notes:** Las Terrenas is remote for serious care.

### Paraguay (added: territorial, easy residency)

- **Update (Stage 5):** since July 2026, Resolution DNM 407/2026 requires proof of income-generating activity or an actual pension to convert to permanent residence; a portfolio-only retiree may need the Investor Pass (US$200k in real estate or financial instruments held 2 years). https://www.imidaily.com/latam-linc/paraguay-ties-long-term-residency-to-active-income-unless-you-invest/

- **1. Wealth tax:** None ("NA"). https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/quick-charts/net-wealth-worth-tax-rates
- **2. Inheritance/estate tax:** None ("NA"). No US treaty exists. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/quick-charts/inheritance-and-gift-tax-rates
- **3. Dividends/interest/foreign income:**
  - Personal income tax (IRP) applies only to "rentas de fuente paraguaya" (Law 6380/2019, art. 48). The capital-income source rules cover deposits and loans "en el país," interest paid by Paraguayan residents and dividends from Paraguayan entities. The rules that treat foreign interest and dividends as taxable sit in the business tax (IRE), not IRP.
  - The capital-income rate is 8% (art. 60).
  - Foreign brokerage dividends and interest therefore appear untaxed. Secondary sources disagree, so get local confirmation.
  - https://www.bacn.gov.py/leyes-paraguayas/9332/ley-n-6380-de-modernizacion-y-simplificacion-del-sistema-tributario-nacional ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/paraguay/individual/taxes-on-personal-income
- **4. Residency on passive income or assets:** Temporary residency of up to 2 years (extendable) is a prerequisite for permanent residency. The fee is Gs. 2,926,925. No proof-of-means amount is published on the page. https://www.migraciones.gov.py/index.php/tramites/residencia-temporal
- **5. Healthcare:** Not researched. Asunción is the only realistic base for good private care; this is **unverified**.
- **BBD verdict:** On paper the strongest tax profile: territorial, no wealth tax, no inheritance tax. Healthcare and lifestyle are weaker, and US-source dividends are likely untaxed but should be confirmed.

- **US estate/gift tax treaties.** The US has them with only 15 countries: Australia, Austria, Denmark, France, Germany, Japan, UK (estate and gift); Canada, Finland, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Netherlands, South Africa, Switzerland (estate only). None of Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia or the Philippines is on the list. https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-gift-tax-treaties-international
- **US income tax treaties.** Thailand, Indonesia and the Philippines have one. Malaysia and Vietnam do not. https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-states-income-tax-treaties-a-to-z
- **US worldwide tax still applies.** As a US citizen you stay taxed by the US on worldwide income wherever you live. Local tax on dividends is on top of that and is relieved only through foreign tax credits. (This is general US law, not from a fetched source.)

### Malaysia (Kuala Lumpur, Penang)

1. **Wealth tax.** None. PwC: "There are no net wealth/worth taxes in Malaysia." https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/malaysia/individual/other-taxes
2. **Inheritance/estate tax.** None. PwC: "There are no inheritance, estate, or gift taxes in Malaysia." There is no US estate tax treaty, but with no Malaysian estate tax there is nothing to double-tax. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/malaysia/individual/other-taxes ; https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-gift-tax-treaties-international
3. **Dividends, interest and foreign income.**
   - Foreign-sourced income received in Malaysia by resident individuals is exempt, subject to conditions, for income received from 1 Jan 2022 to **31 Dec 2036**. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/malaysia/individual/income-determination
   - Dividends: single-tier dividends are exempt. From YA 2025, annual dividend income above MYR 100,000 is taxed at 2%. My reading is that this hits Malaysian-company dividends, while foreign dividends fall under the foreign-income exemption; confirm with an adviser. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/malaysia/individual/income-determination
   - Interest on deposits at approved Malaysian institutions is exempt. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/malaysia/individual/income-determination
   - Tax residence: 182 days or more in a calendar year. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/malaysia/individual/residence
   - MM2H site: participants get a "tax exemption on foreign funds or income". https://www.mm2h.gov.my/apply/guidelines
   - Loan proceeds are not income, so nothing in the regime would tax remitting them; this is irrelevant given the exemption.
4. **Residency routes (MM2H).**
   - Tiers are Silver, Gold, Platinum and SEZ/SFZ, from age 25 (SEZ/SFZ from 21). The fixed deposit must be with a Malaysian licensed bank.
   - Buying a home is compulsory and it cannot be sold for 10 years, except to upgrade.
   - Up to 50% of the FD can be withdrawn after approval for a home, education, medical or tourism costs.
   - Under-50s must stay 90 days a year; there is no minimum stay from age 50.
   - Annual pass fee is RM500.
   - Source: https://www.mm2h.gov.my/apply/guidelines (the official per-tier figures are images I could not read).

   Per-tier figures from a secondary source (Asia Lifestyle Magazine, "as at October 2026"):

   | Tier | Fixed deposit | Pass length | Minimum home value | Participation fee |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | Silver | USD 150k | 5 yrs | RM600k | RM1k |
   | Gold | USD 500k | 15 yrs | RM1m | RM3k |
   | Platinum | USD 1m | 20 yrs | RM2m | RM200k |
   | SEZ/SFZ | USD 65k (USD 32k at 50+) | 10 yrs | RM500k, from the zone developer (Forest City) | not given |

   Kuala Lumpur sets a RM1m floor for foreign residential buyers, and the higher of the state floor and the tier minimum applies. https://www.asialifestylemagazine.com/mm2h-2026-requirements-tiers-deposits-how-to-apply/
   - **Sarawak S-MM2H:** age 30+, RM500,000 FD per application (spouse and dependants included) with a Sarawak bank, 30 days a year in Sarawak, 5-year pass renewable for 5 more. The article is undated as to year; some 2026 guides still quote the older RM150k. https://thesun.my/malaysia-news/sarawak-s-new-requirements-for-s-mm2h-to-take-effect-jan-1-abdul-karim-GC13226088
   - **Penang:** I found no separate Penang programme (PM2H) in 2026 sources. Penang uses federal MM2H plus the state's own foreign-buyer price floor, which I did not verify.
5. **Healthcare.**
   - **Public:** foreigners get no subsidies. A foreign patient's specialist outpatient consult is RM120 against RM1–5 for Malaysians, and deposits are required before treatment (Health Minister, 15 Jun 2026). https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/06/15/foreign-patient-treatment-bills-on-downward-trend-says-dr-dzul
   - **Private:** since 1 Jul 2025, private hospitals charge 6% service tax on care for foreign nationals. Industry asked how this applies to foreigners living in Malaysia, and I did not confirm whether MM2H holders are carved out. https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/personal-finance-news/private-hospitals-ask-mof-to-delay-6-sst-on-foreign-patients.html
   - Insurance premiums at 65–75 and entry-age caps were not verified.

- **BBD verdict:** Best tax fit in the region. There is no wealth, estate or gift tax, and foreign income is exempt through 2036, so dividends left in the US brokerage and loan draws are both untaxed locally. The costs are the MM2H deposit (USD 150k+, partly withdrawable) and the forced home purchase.
- **City notes:** KL's RM1m foreign-buyer floor makes Silver effectively a RM1m-home tier there. Penang's floor was not verified.

### Thailand (Chiang Mai, Bangkok, Hua Hin)

1. **Wealth tax.** None. PwC: "There are no net wealth/worth taxes in Thailand." https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/thailand/individual/other-taxes
2. **Inheritance/estate tax.**
   - Rates: 10% (5% for ascendants/descendants) on the value above THB 100m received per heir. Spouses are exempt. The tax is paid by the heir. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/thailand/individual/other-taxes
   - Who is liable: Thai nationals, and non-Thais "with residence under immigration law", are taxed on inheritances wherever the property sits. Other non-Thai recipients are taxed only on property in Thailand. https://www.forvismazars.com/th/en/insights/doing-business-in-thailand/tax/inheritance-tax-act
   - BBD implication: US-resident non-Thai heirs receiving a US brokerage account are outside the tax. A Thai-resident heir, such as a spouse (exempt anyway) or a child living in Thailand, would be inside it above THB 100m.
   - There is no US estate tax treaty. https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-gift-tax-treaties-international
3. **Dividends, interest and foreign income.**
   - Tax residence: 180 days or more in a calendar year. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/thailand/individual/residence
   - Remittance basis since 2024 (Por. 161/2566): foreign income arising on or after 1 Jan 2024 is taxable whenever it is brought into Thailand. Pre-2024 income is protected by Por. 162/2566 if you can trace it, so keep 31 Dec 2023 statements. https://www.thailawonline.com/thailand-foreign-income-tax-2026-what-actually-applies/
   - Proposed relief, not law: the Revenue Department has drafted an exemption for foreign income remitted in the year earned or the following year. As at 19 Oct 2026 it had not been published in the Royal Gazette and had no timetable. https://www.hlbthai.com/thai-revenue-department-drafts-foreign-income-remittance-tax-relief/ ; https://www.thailawonline.com/thailand-foreign-income-tax-2026-what-actually-applies/
   - LTR exemption: Royal Decree No. 743, s.5, exempts Wealthy Global Citizen, Wealthy Pensioner and Work-from-Thailand Professional LTR holders on Section 40 income "derived in the previous tax year from ... a property situated abroad, and brought into Thailand". That covers foreign dividends and interest. Note the literal "previous tax year" wording, which Revenue Department rules under s.6 govern. https://ltr.boi.go.th/documents/Royal%20Decree%20issued%20under%20the%20Revenue%20Code%20No.743%20(EN).pdf
   - **Loan proceeds:** the remittance charge reaches only "assessable income" under s.40. Borrowed principal is not s.40 income, and principal or capital remittances are generally described as non-assessable.
     - I found no Revenue Department ruling specifically on margin or SBL loan proceeds.
     - BDO's 2023 alert reportedly says the rule "does not apply to ... a loan remitted to Thailand". I saw this only in a search snippet; the page would not load (DNS error), so treat it as unverified.
     - Forvis Mazars notes the Revenue Department has not explained "how to differentiate capital from income". https://www.forvismazars.com/th/en/insights/doing-business-in-thailand/tax/q-a-published-on-foreign-sourced-income
     - Risk: the Revenue Department could argue that a loan secured on, and repaid from, foreign income is a disguised remittance. Keep the loan in a separate account with a clean paper trail.
   - Domestic Thai dividends carry 10% WHT; Thai bank interest carries 15% WHT. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/thailand/individual/income-determination
4. **Residency routes.**
   - **Non-Immigrant O-A (1 year):**
     - Age 50+.
     - Money: THB 800k in a bank, or THB 65k a month income, or a combination totalling THB 800k a year.
     - Health insurance of at least USD 100k / THB 3m including Covid. If insurers refuse you, a USD 100k medical-only deposit is accepted instead.
     - 90-day reporting.
     - https://oslo.thaiembassy.org/en/page/non-immigrant-o-a-long-stay ; https://longstay.tgia.org/guidelineoa
   - **Non-O retirement extension (in-country):** THB 800k or THB 65k a month, no insurance requirement. https://thethaiger.com/insurance/health/retirees/
   - **O-X (10 years):** age 50+, 14 nationalities only, Thai insurance of at least THB 400k inpatient / THB 40k outpatient. The page lists 12 of the 14 countries and the US is not among them, so I could not confirm US eligibility. https://longstay.tgia.org/guidelineox
   - **LTR (10 years), Wealthy Pensioner:** age 50+, USD 80k a year passive income. Alternatively USD 40–80k a year plus a USD 250k Thai investment. Realized gains, dividends and interest count; a BBD holder must actually show this income. https://ltr.boi.go.th/
   - **LTR, Wealthy Global Citizen:** USD 1m in assets plus a USD 500k Thai investment (bonds, property or a company). No age requirement. https://ltr.boi.go.th/
   - **Insurance for both LTR categories:** USD 50k health cover, or Thai social security, or USD 100k in a bank for 12 months. https://ltr.boi.go.th/
5. **Healthcare.**
   - **Public:** foreigners are outside the 30-baht universal scheme and the free UCEP emergency cover, which are for Thais only. https://www.thailandstarterkit.com/health/thailand-health-insurance/
   - **Private hospitals in Bangkok:** Bumrungrad, Bangkok Hospital group, Samitivej, Medpark and others. https://www.thailandstarterkit.com/health/thailand-health-insurance/
   - **Chiang Mai and Hua Hin hospitals:** not verified.
   - **Entry-age caps:** most domestic insurers take new applicants only under 60–65. Pacific Cross takes them up to 75. https://www.pattayamail.com/latestnews/news/547221-547221
   - Other caps: Luma signs up to age 70 (its Long Stay Care plan to under 79), LMG under 80, April International to 60. A Cigna senior plan is described as open to all ages. https://www.thailandstarterkit.com/health/thailand-health-insurance/
   - **Premiums:** THB 28k–175k a year at 60–64 and THB 120k–870k+ a year at 75+. I found no sourced figure for 65–74. https://www.pattayamail.com/latestnews/news/547221-547221

- **BBD verdict:** Workable but fiddly. Leave dividends offshore and remit only loan proceeds, which are probably not assessable income though no ruling confirms it. An LTR Wealthy Pensioner or Global Citizen visa removes remittance risk on foreign income. Inheritance tax is irrelevant for non-resident non-Thai heirs.
- **Note:** O-A insurance and entry-age caps bite hard after 65. Buy cover before 65, or use the in-country Non-O extension, which needs no insurance.

### Vietnam (Da Nang)

1. **Wealth tax.** None. PwC: "There are no net wealth/worth taxes in Vietnam." https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/vietnam/individual/other-taxes
2. **Inheritance/estate tax.**
   - There is no separate estate tax. Inheritances and gifts above VND 20m are taxed as income of the recipient at 10%, for residents and non-residents alike. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/vietnam/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/vietnam/individual/income-determination
   - Whether US heirs receiving US-situs securities from a Vietnam-resident decedent are reached is unclear, and I did not verify it. Because the tax falls on the recipient, a non-resident heir is likely outside it for foreign assets.
   - There is no US estate tax treaty and no US income tax treaty. https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-states-income-tax-treaties-a-to-z
3. **Dividends, interest and foreign income.**
   - Residents are taxed on worldwide income. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/vietnam/individual/taxes-on-personal-income
   - Residence: 183 days in a calendar year or 12 months, or a "permanent residence", which includes a fixed-term rented house. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/vietnam/individual/residence
   - Rates: investment income (dividends, interest) 5%; bank-deposit interest exempt; securities sales 0.1% of proceeds; capital assignment 20% of gain. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/vietnam/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/vietnam/individual/income-determination
   - A new PIT Law takes effect 1 Jul 2026, with some provisions from 1 Jan 2026. I could not see its effect on investment or foreign income. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/vietnam/individual/significant-developments
   - Loan proceeds are not income. With no US treaty, the 5% on US dividends is relieved only through US foreign tax credits.
4. **Residency routes.**
   - There is no retirement visa. A proposed 5/10-year "Golden Visa" covering retirees was not enacted as of mid-2025. https://news.tuoitre.vn/vietnams-retiree-visa-should-attract-financially-stable-individuals-opinion-103250618163103742.htm
   - **90-day e-visa:** USD 25 single entry or USD 50 multiple entry. It is not renewable without a sponsor. https://getwherenext.com/blog/vietnam-retirement-visa-2026-honest-truth
   - **Temporary residence cards** are issued only for ĐT1–ĐT3 investors, the TT family route and certain work categories. Tourist, e-visa and ĐT4 holders cannot get one. https://getwherenext.com/blog/vietnam-retirement-visa-2026-honest-truth
     - ĐT3 (VND 3bn–50bn investment): up to 3 years.
     - ĐT2 (VND 50bn–100bn): up to 5 years.
     - ĐT1 (VND 100bn+): card up to 10 years.
   - The new UĐ1/UĐ2 categories under Law 118/2025, from 1 Jul 2026, are for digital-tech workers, not retirees. https://getwherenext.com/blog/vietnam-retirement-visa-2026-honest-truth
   - In practice retirees use repeated e-visas with border runs, set up a company for an investor visa, or marry a Vietnamese citizen (5-year visa exemption certificate). https://emerhub.com/vietnam/how-to-retire-in-vietnam-as-a-foreign-expat/
5. **Healthcare.**
   - Da Nang and Ho Chi Minh City are described as having modern facilities; Vinmec (national chain) and FV (HCMC) are named as top tier. https://emerhub.com/vietnam/how-to-retire-in-vietnam-as-a-foreign-expat/ ; https://getwherenext.com/blog/vietnam-retirement-visa-2026-honest-truth
   - Insurance is a required document for investor-visa applications. A rough USD 50–200 a month is quoted, not age-specific and low-quality. https://emerhub.com/vietnam/how-to-retire-in-vietnam-as-a-foreign-expat/
   - Public access for foreigners and entry-age caps were not verified.

- **BBD verdict:** Taxes are light (5% on dividends, no wealth or estate tax) but worldwide. There is no US treaty and no legal long-stay route for a pure retiree. Residency is the deal-breaker, not tax.
- **City note:** Da Nang has Vinmec. Serious care may mean HCMC or Bangkok.

### Indonesia (Bali)

1. **Wealth tax.** None. PwC: "There are no net wealth/worth taxes in Indonesia." https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/indonesia/individual/other-taxes
2. **Inheritance/estate tax.** None. PwC: "There are no inheritance, estate, and gift taxes in Indonesia." There is no US estate tax treaty, but there is a US income tax treaty. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/indonesia/individual/other-taxes ; https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-states-income-tax-treaties-a-to-z
3. **Dividends, interest and foreign income.**
   - Residents are taxed on worldwide income at progressive rates. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/indonesia/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://pajak.go.id/en/artikel/tax-return-reporting-foreign-citizens-indonesia
   - Residence: more than 183 days in 12 months, or present with intent to reside. A KITAS is commonly read as intent. https://pajak.go.id/en/artikel/tax-return-reporting-foreign-citizens-indonesia ; https://www.libertymundo.com/retire-in-indonesia/
   - The territorial, foreign-income-exempt regime for four years exists only for foreigners with "certain expertise" (PMK 18/2021 lists job roles). I found no retiree category, so retirees likely do not qualify. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/indonesia/individual/taxes-on-personal-income
   - Local rates: domestic dividends 10% final (exempt if reinvested in Indonesia); bank interest 20% final. Foreign dividends are ordinary income at 5–35%. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/indonesia/individual/income-determination ; https://www.libertymundo.com/retire-in-indonesia/
   - Loan proceeds are not income. Dividends, though, are taxed annually in Indonesia whether remitted or not.
4. **Residency routes.**
   - **Second Home Visa (E33):** USD 130k (IDR 2bn) deposit at a state-owned bank, or a USD 1m apartment. 5 or 10 years, no age limit. https://emerhub.com/indonesia/visas/retirement-visa/ ; https://www.aseanbriefing.com/news/indonesia-to-issue-second-home-visa-what-are-the-requirements/
   - **Retirement KITAS (E33F):**
     - Age 55+, though a few listings still say 60.
     - 1-year permit renewed annually; KITAP (permanent residence) after about 5 years.
     - Needs a sponsor or agent, and hiring Indonesian household staff.
     - Income requirement conflicts: USD 3,000 a month per Emerhub, USD 1,500 a month per LibertyMundo.
     - https://emerhub.com/indonesia/visas/retirement-visa/ ; https://www.libertymundo.com/retire-in-indonesia/
   - **Silver Hair (E33E):** 5 years, no sponsor, USD 50k deposit at a state bank. https://emerhub.com/indonesia/visas/retirement-visa/
5. **Healthcare.**
   - Private hospitals in Bali include BIMC and Siloam.
   - The retirement KITAS insurance minimum is IDR 25m; USD 50–100k cover is advised.
   - International cover runs about USD 2,500–6,000 a year for ages 55–70.
   - Optional enrolment in the public scheme (BPJS) is about USD 12 a month.
   - https://www.libertymundo.com/retire-in-indonesia/
   - Entry-age caps were not verified.

- **BBD verdict:** No wealth or estate tax is good, but residents are taxed on worldwide income including US dividends at up to 35%, and retirees get no foreign-income exemption. Workable only with a low-dividend portfolio. Visas are easy: a USD 130k deposit, or a USD 50k Silver Hair deposit.
- **City note:** Bali private care is fine for routine needs; complex cases often go to Jakarta or Singapore (not verified).

### Philippines (added: strong residency route, but watch the estate tax)

1. **Wealth tax.** Not verified (PwC's "other taxes" page does not cover it).
2. **Inheritance/estate tax.** Flat 6% on the net estate, covering "the worldwide estate of Filipino citizens and resident aliens". The standard deduction is PHP 5m. https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/estate-tax-computation-guidelines-in-the-philippines
   - This is the key BBD red flag. A resident-alien decedent's US brokerage would face 6% Philippine estate tax on top of possible US estate tax.
   - There is no US estate tax treaty; US relief would come only through the US credit for foreign death taxes (not verified here). https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-gift-tax-treaties-international
3. **Income.** Resident aliens are taxed on Philippine-source income at graduated rates; foreign-source exclusion is implied, not stated. Domestic dividends are taxed at 10%. A US income tax treaty exists. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/philippines/individual/income-determination ; https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-states-income-tax-treaties-a-to-z
4. **SRRV, Expanded program (PRA, Aug 2025).**
   - Age 40+.
   - Classic deposit: USD 30k at 50+ without a pension, or USD 15k at 50+ with a pension of USD 800 a month (single) / USD 1,000 (family).
   - USD 1,500 processing fee; USD 360 annual fee.
   - https://pra.gov.ph/Uploads/Files/Expanded%20SRRV%20Program.pdf
5. **Healthcare.** Not researched.

- **BBD verdict:** Cheapest, easiest permanent visa, and foreign income is likely untaxed. But the 6% worldwide estate tax on resident aliens directly hits the "die" leg of BBD. Fine for living, bad for dying there as a resident.

### 2. US estate tax for US citizens abroad

- **2026 basic exclusion amount: $15,000,000 per person** [P]:
  - set by P.L. 119-21, signed 4 Jul 2025, which the IRS refers to as the "One, Big, Beautiful Bill" (OBBB) and on another page as the "Working Families Tax Cuts" bill;
  - it amends §2010(c)(3);
  - the 2025 amount was $13,990,000;
  - the annual gift exclusion for 2026 is $19,000.
  - https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/whats-new-estate-and-gift-tax ; https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill
- Indexing [P]:
  - §2010(c)(3)(A) is now a permanent $15,000,000;
  - (B) indexes it for decedents dying after 2026, using 2025 as the base year, rounded to $10,000;
  - there is no sunset;
  - the IRS had not published the 2027 figure in anything I found; third-party projections are about $15.3–15.5M.
  - https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/2010
- US citizens are subject to estate tax on their worldwide gross estate regardless of where they live. This is standard law; §2001 taxes "every decedent who is a citizen or resident," but I did not fetch a page quoting that text, so cite §2001/§2031 directly if needed.
- **Step-up in basis** [P]: under §1014(a)(1), basis becomes fair market value at the date of death for property acquired from a decedent ((b)(1) bequest/inheritance; (b)(9) property included in the estate). The statute has no condition based on the decedent's residence or domicile, so a citizen dying abroad still gets the step-up on US brokerage holdings. — https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1014
- **US estate and gift tax treaties in force (IRS list, 15 countries)** [P]:
  - estate and gift: Australia, Austria, Denmark, France, Germany, Japan, UK;
  - estate only: Canada (Art. XXIX B of the income tax treaty), Finland, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Netherlands, South Africa, Switzerland.
  - In scope, only **France, Greece and Italy** have estate treaties. **None** exist with Portugal, Spain, Croatia, Montenegro, Cyprus, Malta, or any Latin American or Southeast Asian country in scope.
  - https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-gift-tax-treaties-international
- **§2014 foreign death tax credit (non-treaty countries)** [P]:
  - the credit covers foreign estate, inheritance, legacy or succession tax "actually paid to any foreign country in respect of any property **situated within such foreign country**" and included in the gross estate;
  - situs is determined under the nonresident rules (§2101 et seq.);
  - two caps apply: a per-tax pro-rata cap and an overall cap equal to the share of US tax attributable to foreign-situs property;
  - it must be claimed within 4 years of filing.
  - https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/2014
- **US securities are US-situs** [P]: "Shares of stock issued by a domestic corporation, irrespective of the location of the certificates" are US-situated. — https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/20.2104-1
- **Consequence:**
  - If a country of residence without a treaty (e.g. Spain, which has a regional inheritance tax) taxes the heirs on US stocks, §2014 gives **no US credit**, because the property is not situated in that foreign country. Double taxation results unless the foreign country gives its own unilateral credit (not researched here).
  - At a $15M exemption, US estate tax is often zero anyway, so the foreign tax is usually the binding one.
  - In treaty countries (France, Greece, Italy) the treaty's situs and credit rules apply instead. Treaty texts were not read.
  - §2014(h) lets the President deny the credit to citizens of countries that do not reciprocate.
  - https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/2014 ; https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/20.2104-1

  - REIBP (one-off advance payment covering 2023–2027, with stability to 2038) was open only to people who were Argentine tax residents on 31 Dec 2023. A new arrival can't use it.
  - The FY2026 scale (0.5–0.75%?) is `[unverified]`.

  - PBA scale for children/spouse: about 1.6%–7.0% (2024 law, indexed yearly).
  - 2026 allowance for parents/children/spouse: ARS 23.3M per a search snippet `[unverified]`.

### Chile (Santiago)

- **Wealth tax:** none. "There are no net wealth/worth taxes in Chile." A wealth tax has been proposed repeatedly but not enacted; a 2026 bill's status is `[unverified]`. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/chile/individual/other-taxes

So a foreign national's US brokerage, bought with non-Chilean money, is outside the tax. Chilean-situs assets are taxed.
  - Foreign estate tax paid on included assets is credited.
  - No US estate treaty.

  - US–Chile income tax treaty exists.
  - https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/chile/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/chile/individual/income-determination ; https://www.sii.cl/normativa_legislacion/jurisprudencia_administrativa/ley_impuesto_renta/2016/ja2405.htm ; https://www.sii.cl/valores_y_fechas/impuesto_2da_categoria/impuesto2026.htm

  - Temporary residence as a rentista or pensionado/jubilado. There is **no fixed minimum**; in practice show more than about US$1,000/month.
  - The first permit is about 1 year, renewable. Permanent residence is possible after 2 years, with no more than 180 days abroad.

After year 3, US dividends are taxed at up to 40%, so a dividend-light (growth) portfolio matters.
- **City notes:** Santiago (Las Condes, Vitacura, Providencia) has top hospitals, stable institutions and high living costs for the region. Air pollution in winter. Earthquake risk.

### Georgia (Tbilisi; also Batumi)

1. **Wealth tax:** none. Georgia has an annual property tax on real estate, cars and yachts, but only for families with income over GEL 40,000; income that is exempt (foreign income) is presumably excluded **[unverified]**. https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/georgia/tax/georgia-tax-card-2025 ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/georgia/individual/other-taxes
2. **Inheritance/estate tax:** none as a separate tax. Property inherited or gifted by first- and second-line heirs (children, spouse, parents, siblings, grandchildren) is exempt from personal income tax. Gifts up to GEL 1,000 a year are also exempt. There is no US estate tax treaty. https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/georgia/tax/georgia-tax-card-2025 ; https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-gift-tax-treaties-international
3. **Dividends, interest and foreign income:** income of residents from foreign sources is exempt. PwC: "Resident individuals are exempt from tax on income that does not have a Georgian source." The flat PIT rate is 20%. Georgian-source dividends and interest are taxed at 5% final withholding. US dividends would therefore carry only the US 30% non-resident withholding, since there is no US–Georgia income tax treaty **[unverified]**. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/georgia/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/georgia/tax/georgia-tax-card-2025
   - Caution: one Big-4-adjacent guide (Eurofast) says residents are taxed on "global income". PwC and the 2025 tax card both list foreign-source income as exempt. https://eurofast.eu/understanding-individual-taxes-in-georgia/
4. **Residency routes:** a temporary residence permit by real estate. The threshold rose from **USD 100k to USD 150k (market value) on 1 Mar 2026**, and multiple properties can be combined. The permit renews annually and covers the spouse and children. Holders under the old USD 100k rule can keep renewing. A USD 300k investment track leads to a 5-year and then a permanent permit **[secondary, not confirmed on an official page]**. Most nationalities, including the US, get 1 year visa-free **[unverified]**. https://eiglaw.com/georgia-to-increase-real-estate-investment-threshold-for-residence-permits-effective-march-1-2026/ **[secondary]**
5. **Healthcare:** public coverage is limited. Private Tbilisi hospitals (American Hospital Tbilisi and others) are modern and English-speaking, but quality falls off outside the capital. Local policies are cheaper than international ones. No age-specific pricing was found, so budget the international benchmark of roughly €4–7k/yr at 65–69. https://www.april-international.com/en/destinations/asia-pacific/health-insurance-in-georgia **[secondary]**
- **BBD verdict:** Near-ideal on tax: foreign income is exempt, there is no inheritance tax for children, and there is no wealth tax, so the only tax drag is US withholding on dividends. The weak points are healthcare depth for 70+ serious care and geopolitical and stability risk.
- **City notes:** Tbilisi has the best hospitals and most expats. Batumi is a Black Sea resort city with weaker medical care and a property market (the residence route) with an oversupply of new builds **[unverified]**.

### Hungary (Budapest)

1. **Wealth tax:** "There are no net wealth/worth taxes in Hungary." https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/hungary/individual/other-taxes
2. **Inheritance/estate tax:** the general rate is 18%, or 9% for residential property. Transfers between lineal relatives (children) and to a surviving spouse are **exempt**. PwC does not detail how far the tax reaches foreign assets of a resident; with the lineal exemption this is moot for children. There is no US estate tax treaty. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/hungary/individual/other-taxes
3. **Dividends, interest and foreign income:** residents are taxed on worldwide income. PIT is 15% on almost everything, with dividends and interest taxed separately. On top of that comes a **13% social contribution tax (szocho)** on interest and on dividends, except dividends from EEA-listed companies. US-listed dividends are therefore in scope. The szocho base is capped at 24× the monthly minimum wage, about HUF 7.75m in 2026 (my calculation), reduced by other szocho-liable income. The effective rate is up to about 28% until the cap, then 15%. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/hungary/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/hungary/individual/other-taxes
4. **Residency routes:** the guest investor residence permit takes ≥ EUR 250k in an MNB-registered real estate fund held 5 years, or a ≥ EUR 1m donation to a higher-education institution. It is valid up to 10 years and renewable for 10 more, comes with a family reunification permit, and takes 21 days to process. Direct property purchase is not listed. https://oif.gov.hu/factsheets/residence-permit-for-guest-investor
5. **Healthcare:** public cover depends on employment, contributions and residency. Without a TAJ card you pay full private rates even in public hospitals. A reader reported a non-EU resident refused a TAJ card four times **[anecdotal]**. Private GP visits cost HUF 20–35k. Public waits are long but emergency care is "generally reliable". Plan on international private insurance at the benchmark above. https://dailynewshungary.com/moving-to-hungary-expats-healthcare-insurance **[secondary]**
- **BBD verdict:** Inheritance to children is tax-free, but holding is expensive: 15% PIT plus up to 13% szocho on US dividends and interest. That is a moderate drag, and the €250k fund lock-up is the cost of entry.
- **City notes:** Budapest is the only realistic base, with the best private clinics, an international airport and EU Schengen access.

### Czech Republic (Prague)

1. **Wealth tax:** "no net wealth/worth taxes in the Czech Republic." https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/czech-republic/individual/other-taxes
2. **Inheritance/estate tax:** "There is no inheritance tax." Gifts are taxed as income, with an exemption for gifts from close relatives. There is no US estate tax treaty. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/czech-republic/individual/other-taxes
3. **Dividends, interest and foreign income:** residents are taxed on worldwide income. Foreign dividends and bond interest can go in a **separate tax base at a flat 15%**, or be taxed at the 15%/23% progressive rates (23% above CZK 1,762,812). A credit for US withholding is presumably available under the US–CZ treaty **[unverified]**. Capital gains on securities are exempt after 3 years of holding, and the CZK 40m annual cap was abolished from 1 Jan 2026. That helps if BBD ever sells. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/czech-republic/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/czech-republic/individual/income-determination
4. **Residency routes:** there is no retirement or investor visa. The route is a long-term visa for "other purposes" (up to 12 months, decided by the Ministry of the Interior in 90–120 days). It requires a written purpose statement, proof of funds of 15× the CZK 3,130 subsistence minimum plus 2× per extra month, accommodation, and comprehensive health insurance of at least EUR 400k per event with no co-pay. This is the Tel Aviv embassy's list; other embassies are similar. Renewal into a long-term residence permit is discretionary **[unverified]**. https://mzv.gov.cz/telaviv/en/visa_and_consular_services/visas/long_term_visas_and_residence_permits/long_term_visa_for_other_purposes.html
5. **Healthcare:** non-EU residents without employment must buy commercial "comprehensive" insurance. Since Sept 2023 the market is open beyond PVZP, and the limit for long-term residents is EUR 400k. No age-banded premiums were found; PVZP is said to be the most expensive. Hospital quality in Prague is good **[unverified]**. https://expats.cz/czech-news/article/pavel-signs-law-ending-pvzp-health-monopoly-for-non-eu-foreigners **[secondary]**
- **BBD verdict:** Clean on estate (no inheritance tax), with a flat 15% option on US dividends and interest and a 3-year capital gains exemption. The main friction is the discretionary "other purposes" visa and getting senior-age insurance.
- **City notes:** Prague has the best hospitals and expat services and a good airport. Brno is a cheaper fallback.

### Turkey (Istanbul; also Antalya)

1. **Wealth tax:** "There are no national wealth taxes in Turkey." A high-value residence tax applies to Turkish homes worth over TRY 17.711m in 2026, at 0.3–1.0% of the excess. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/turkey/individual/other-taxes
2. **Inheritance/estate tax:** inheritance and gift tax is 1–30%, with foreign tax paid deductible from the base. **New (Law 7582, gazetted 4 Jun 2026):** people using the foreign-income exemption pay a reduced 1% on inheritances during the 20-year period. Sources disagree on whether this keys off the decedent or the heir. Under Turkish law the tax reaches Turkish citizens' worldwide property plus property in Turkey; a foreign resident's US securities left to US heirs are likely out of scope **[secondary/unverified]**. The 2026 exemption for children is about TRY 2.9m per heir **[unverified, LinkedIn only]**. There is no US estate tax treaty. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/turkey/individual/other-taxes ; https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/turkiye-introduces-new-personal-tax-regime-including-foreign-income-exemption-inheritance-tax-relief-and-asset-repatriation-measure ; https://www.ibanet.org/New-tax-developments-in-Turkey-2026
3. **Dividends, interest and foreign income:** residents (more than 6 months a year) are normally taxed on worldwide income at 15–40%, and foreign dividends and interest over TRY 22k must be declared. **Law 7582** gives a **20-year income tax exemption on foreign-source income** for people becoming resident on or after 1 Jan 2026. Conditions:
   - no Turkish residence or tax liability in the prior 3 calendar years;
   - apply to the tax office for an exemption certificate by the end of the year residence starts, or by end-February if residence starts in November or December;
   - exempt income is not reported, and no foreign tax credit is given.
   Whether foreign capital gains are covered is not stated explicitly; the "income and gains derived from abroad" wording suggests yes. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/turkey/individual/income-determination ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/turkey/individual/residence ; https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/turkey/tax/turkiyes-new-foreign-income-exemption-a-regime-for-relocating-individuals **[law firm]**
4. **Residency routes:** a short-term residence permit by property ownership needs **≥ USD 200k** assessed value for purchases from 16 Oct 2023 (older titles: USD 75k metro, 50k elsewhere). It lasts 1–2 years and is renewable while you own the property. First applications are banned in 1,169 "closed" neighborhoods in 15 provinces. Applicants aged 18–64 must show health insurance. Permanent residence is possible after 8 years, citizenship after 5. https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/turkey/real-estate-and-construction/residence-permit-in-turkey-by-buying-real-estate-%E2%80%93-legal-advice **[law firm]**
5. **Healthcare:** after 1 year of residence foreigners can join SGK/GSS voluntarily at 24% of the minimum wage. Over-65s are exempt from the private insurance requirement for the permit. Since 2017 SGK excludes pre-existing conditions for these enrollees, so top-up cover is advised. Private hospitals in Istanbul and Antalya are major medical-tourism hubs **[unverified]**. https://ikamet.com/health-care-services-for-foreigners-in-turkey/ **[secondary]**
- **BBD verdict:** Newly attractive: a 20-year foreign-income exemption from 2026 makes US dividends untaxed locally, and inheritance exposure on US assets looks minimal. The risks are an untested regime, the lira and inflation, and policy volatility. You must file for the certificate in year 1.
- **City notes:** Istanbul has the best hospitals, but many districts are closed to new residence permits. Antalya is a cheaper coastal base with good private hospitals, though some neighborhoods are closed too. Check the DGMM list before buying.

### Ireland (Dublin)

1. **Wealth tax:** "currently no net wealth/worth taxes on individuals in Ireland." https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/ireland/individual/other-taxes
2. **Inheritance/estate tax:** Capital Acquisitions Tax (CAT) is **33%** above the Group A (child) threshold, which is **€420,000 for gifts or inheritances on or after 7 Oct 2026** (€400k from 2 Oct 2024 to 6 Oct 2026). CAT applies if the disponer or the beneficiary is resident or ordinarily resident, regardless of domicile. A **non-domiciled** person counts as resident only after **5 consecutive years** of residence. After year 5, a US decedent's worldwide estate passing to US children falls into CAT. The **US–Ireland estate tax treaty** gives credit relief. https://www.revenue.ie/en/gains-gifts-and-inheritance/cat-thresholds-rates-and-aggregation-rules/cat-thresholds.aspx ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/ireland/individual/other-taxes ; https://www.irishtimes.com/business/personal-finance/what-are-the-tax-rules-on-bringing-money-back-into-ireland-1.4773102 **[secondary, quoting Revenue rule]** ; https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-gift-tax-treaties-international
3. **Dividends, interest and foreign income:** non-domiciled residents are taxed on foreign investment income and foreign gains **only to the extent remitted** to Ireland. A US citizen normally keeps a US domicile of origin. Remitted income is taxed at marginal rates up to 40% plus USC **[unverified]**. Capital gains tax is 33%, and 40% on certain fund interests. Remitting loan proceeds secured on unremitted foreign income may be treated as a remittance; this needs advice **[unverified]**. Residence is 183 days a year or 280 days over two years. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/ireland/individual/income-determination ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/ireland/individual/residence
4. **Residency routes:** **Stamp 0** (retirement / independent means) requires at least €50k a year per person (€100k for a couple) from pensions or savings ("speculative investments generally not considered"). You also need a lump sum for emergencies, full private health insurance and an accountant's certification. It is granted for 1 year and renewed annually, with no access to State benefits. https://www.lewissilkin.com/en-ie/insights/2025/08/07/stamp-0irelands-visa-for-retirees-or-persons-of-independent-means **[law firm]**
5. **Healthcare:** Stamp 0 holders must hold private cover, which is community-rated (no age pricing and no entry-age cap). First-time buyers over 34 pay a **Lifetime Community Rating loading of 2% per year over 34, capped at 70%** (age 69+), for up to 10 years. A 9-month grace period applies only to people abroad on 1 May 2015 who later return. Hospital quality is high, but public waits are long. https://www.layahealthcare.ie/questions/findoutaboutlcr/
- **BBD verdict:** Workable for the first 5 years: the remittance basis lets income stay offshore and CAT doesn't apply yet. After 5 years of residence, 33% CAT hits the worldwide estate, with only treaty credit against US estate tax. Leave before year 5 or plan for it.
- **City notes:** Dublin has the best hospitals and is very expensive. Cork and Galway are cheaper, and the tax treatment is the same.

### Taiwan (Taipei)

1. **Wealth tax:** none ("no net wealth or net worth tax"). https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/taiwan/individual/other-taxes
2. **Inheritance/estate tax:** the estate tax is 10% / 15% / 20%, with the top rate above TWD 112.42m of net estate. The exemption is TWD 13.33m, plus a TWD 560k deduction per lineal descendant. **For non-ROC nationals the estate tax reaches only property located in Taiwan** (Estate and Gift Tax Act Art. 1). Worldwide reach applies only to ROC nationals habitually resident, including more than 365 days' residence in the 2 years before death (Art. 4). A US citizen's US brokerage account is therefore outside Taiwan estate tax. There is no US estate tax treaty. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/taiwan/individual/other-taxes ; https://law.moj.gov.tw/ENG/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0340072
3. **Dividends, interest and foreign income:** regular income tax is territorial (Taiwan-source only). Residents (183+ days) are subject to the **Income Basic Tax (AMT)**:
   - foreign-source income is added back only if it is ≥ TWD 1m;
   - the tax is (basic income − TWD 7.5m) × 20%, payable only to the extent it exceeds regular tax;
   - a foreign tax credit is allowed, so US withholding offsets it.
   Up to about TWD 7.5m (roughly USD 230k) of foreign income a year is effectively untaxed **[FX unverified]**. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/taiwan/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/taiwan/individual/residence
4. **Residency routes:** there is no retirement or passive-income visa, and none was found in any source. The **Resident Visa for Investment** requires a minimum investment of **USD 200,000** proven by ROC authorities (MOEA-approved, i.e. a business investment, not a portfolio). You cannot switch to it after entering visa-free. An ARC is issued within 30 days of arrival. Other routes: the Employment Gold Card or the Entrepreneur visa **[not researched]**. https://www.roc-taiwan.org/uploads/sites/76/2023/04/14.-Resident-Visas-for-Investment-20240731-updated.pdf (TECO, updated 2024-07-31)
5. **Healthcare:** ARC holders without an employer join the NHI after 6 months of continuous residence (one trip under 30 days allowed). The premium is about 4.69% of the insured-salary basis when self-paid, and you keep paying while abroad. Hospital quality is high **[unverified]**. https://taiwangoldcard.com/goldcard-holders-faq/health-insurance/ **[secondary]**
- **BBD verdict:** Excellent on estate (only Taiwan-situs assets are taxed for foreigners) and mild on income (20% AMT only above TWD 7.5m, with foreign tax credit). The NHI is cheap and good. The obstacle is immigration: there is no passive route, so you need a USD 200k business investment or a Gold Card.
- **City notes:** Taipei has the top hospitals (NTUH, Taipei Veterans General), good transit and moderate costs **[unverified]**.

### Japan (Tokyo / Osaka)

1. **Wealth tax:** "There are currently no net wealth/worth taxes in Japan." https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/japan/individual/other-taxes
2. **Inheritance/estate tax:** inheritance and gift tax runs up to **55%** (above JPY 600m of taxable amount). The basic exemption is JPY 30m plus JPY 6m per statutory heir. **Foreign nationals** are exempt on overseas assets only if they have had a Japan domicile for **less than 10 of the last 15 years** and hold a "Table 1" status (work-type or designated activities). PwC notes that permanent residents and spouses or children of permanent residents or Japanese nationals are not Table 1. After 10 years, worldwide assets are taxed at up to 55%. The **US–Japan estate and gift tax treaty** gives credit relief. Japan also has an exit tax on securities **[unverified, not fetched]**. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/japan/individual/other-taxes ; https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-gift-tax-treaties-international
3. **Dividends, interest and foreign income:**
   - **Non-permanent residents** (foreigners resident 5 years or less of the last 10) pay tax on foreign-source income only to the extent it is paid in or remitted to Japan. How remittances funded by borrowing are treated needs advice **[unverified]**.
   - Permanent residents are taxed on worldwide income. Listed-share dividends and capital gains (including foreign-listed shares) can be taxed at a flat 20.315%. Offshore interest is taxed at progressive rates up to 45% plus 2.1% surtax plus 10% inhabitant tax.
   https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/japan/individual/residence ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/japan/individual/taxes-on-personal-income ; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/japan/individual/income-determination
4. **Residency routes:** **there is no retirement visa.**
   - **Long Stay for sightseeing and recreation (Designated Activities):** age 18+, savings above JPY 30m (JPY 60m for a couple applying separately), private medical travel insurance. Stay is 6 months, extendable once to 12 months maximum. You must then leave and can reapply from abroad. It does not count toward permanent residence. https://www.vfsglobal.com/one-pager/japan/singapore/english/pdf/Designated-activities-Long-Stay-Updated.pdf (Embassy requirements via VFS, 26 Sep 2025) ; https://migaku.com/blog/language-fun/retirement-visa-options-in-japan-whats-actually-available **[secondary]**
   - **Business Manager:** from 16 Oct 2025 it requires JPY 30m capital, at least 1 full-time employee and 3 years of management experience or a degree. That is not passive. https://www.morganlewis.com/fr/pubs/2025/09/japanese-government-to-tighten-requirements-for-business-manager-work-permit
5. **Healthcare:** Long Stay holders cannot join National Health Insurance and must carry private insurance for the whole stay. International plans have entry-age caps around 65–70 for many insurers (benchmark above). Hospital quality is world-class **[unverified]**. https://migaku.com/blog/language-fun/retirement-visa-options-in-japan-whats-actually-available **[secondary]**

### Albania (Sarandë; also Tirana)

1. **Wealth tax:** PwC lists none. The only annual levy on assets is a real-estate tax on buildings: 0.05% of value for residential property, 0.2% for commercial. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/albania/individual/other-taxes
2. **Inheritance tax:**
   - The rate is 15% on inheritances and gifts, with no deduction for costs.
   - Inheritances "from/or between the legal heirs in the first and second place" are exempt, and so are transfers between siblings. Children are first-degree heirs, so in practice the children pay 0%.
   - Other recipients get an exempt allowance of ALL 5m for immovable assets and ALL 1m for movable assets.
   - It is a tax on the recipient (heir-based).
   - PwC does not define whether it reaches foreign assets.
   - There is no US estate treaty. Because the children are exempt, a treaty matters little here [inference].
   - https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/albania/individual/other-taxes ; https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-gift-tax-treaties-international
3. **Tax on investment income:**
   - Albania taxes residents on worldwide income: "Resident individuals are taxed on all sources of income in and outside the territory of Albania." https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/albania/individual/taxes-on-personal-income
   - **Dividends 8%.** Other investment income, including interest, **15%**. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/albania/individual/taxes-on-personal-income
   - Capital gains on shares are taxed at 15% on the difference between sale and purchase price. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/albania/individual/income-determination
   - PwC lists no special regime for new residents or pensioners.
   - A 2020 draft law would have exempted foreign pensions for EU pensioners only. Its status is unknown and it never covered dividends. https://www.tiranatimes.com/new-draft-law-proposed-by-govt-stipulates-abolition-of-pension-tax-for-foreigners-in-albania/ [secondary]
   - Albania gives a foreign tax credit capped at the Albanian tax on the same income [secondary, via search; Eurofast and Karanović pages not fetched].
   - There is no US–Albania income tax treaty. **[inference]** The US tax a citizen pays on US dividends (15–23.8%) exceeds 8%, so if Albania credits it, the net Albanian tax on dividends may be close to nil. This is not confirmed.
   - Margin-loan draws are not income. Under BBD the realised gains stay at zero.
4. **Residency routes:**
   - **Visa-free stay of up to 1 year for US citizens.** In March 2022 the Council of Ministers restored this right. A new one-year term needs more than 90 days outside Albania first. https://exit.al/en/albanian-government-does-u-turn-on-us-residency-requirements [secondary, news]
   - **Pensioner permit (Law on Foreigners 79/2021):**
     - Needs an annual pension of at least **ALL 1,200,000** (~€9,800 in 2021, ~€12k at 2026 rates [inference]).
     - The pension must be paid into an Albanian bank. Applicants also need health insurance, a notarised lease or ownership deed, and a criminal record check.
     - The first permit is for 1 year. The article expects renewals of 2, 2 and 5 years, then permanent residence.
     - It needs a **pension**. Portfolio income alone does not qualify.
     - https://exit.al/en/approved-law-on-foreigners-paves-way-for-pensioner-and-digital-nomad-immigrants-in-albania ; https://www.imidaily.com/albania-pensioners-residency/ [secondary]
   - **Property route:**
     - IMI Daily reports a January 2026 amendment (Law 43/2025) that "some legal sources" say sets **€300k in real estate** or €100k in business or government securities. IMI has not confirmed these numbers.
     - The initial permit is for 1 year and is renewable.
     - Older sources say any home of at least 20 m² qualified, with no price floor.
     - **[unverified]**: confirm the current threshold with a lawyer.
     - https://www.imidaily.com/analysis/how-to-get-residency-in-the-balkans-through-investment/ [secondary]
5. **Healthcare:**
   - The UK embassy list of English-speaking facilities (updated Mar 2026) names American Hospital Tirana, German Hospital Tirana, Hygea Hospital Tirana and the public QSUT "Nënë Tereza". It also lists American Hospitals in Durrës and Fier. **It lists nothing in Sarandë.** https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/albania-list-of-medical-facilities/list-of-english-speaking-medical-facilities-for-british-nationals-in-albania--2
   - Sarandë has a public regional hospital for stabilisation [unverified; expatexchange page blocked]. Tirana is about 4–5 h by road, and Corfu General Hospital is ~30–45 min by ferry [unverified, search snippets].
   - No public-system figures for foreign retirees were found.
   - Private international cover is needed for the permit anyway. Use Cigna Global, which has no upper entry age (~$6.5–9.4k/yr at 70–79, Appendix E §3).
6. **Broker / margin:**
   - IBKR: likely, since it reportedly serves all non-sanctioned countries (Appendix E §1).
   - Schwab One International: Albania is not on the checked list of accepted countries, so treat it as **unknown / probably no**.
   - Albania is not in the EU, so the EU limit on cash-out via margin may not apply. Unknown.

**BBD verdict:** No wealth tax, the children inherit free, and dividends are taxed at 8% (possibly near zero after credit for US tax) with gains untaxed while unrealised. A strong BBD tax profile. The weak points are the residence route (pension-based, or a property threshold still unclear) and healthcare in Sarandë, where serious care means Tirana or Corfu.

**City notes:**
- **Safety (UK FCDO):** crimes targeting foreigners are "rare". There is petty theft in tourist areas. Terrorism "cannot be ruled out". There are occasional violent demonstrations in central Tirana. https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/albania/safety-and-security (The US State Department page refused automated fetches.)
- **Climate [unverified]:** Sarandë is Mediterranean, with hot dry summers and mild wet winters. Tirana has hotter summers and cooler winters.
- **Flights:** no confirmed US nonstop for 2026. Reports of seasonal United Newark–Tirana service and a proposed Delta route could not be confirmed [unverified]. Sarandë's nearest airport for most people is Corfu (ferry), or Tirana. Search snippets suggested Corfu has no US nonstops [unverified].
- **Expat community:** nearly 3,000 Italian pensioners had registered as Albanian residents by Oct 2023. https://top-channel.tv/english/over-three-thousand-italian-retirees-registered-as-residents-in-albania [secondary, news]. Sarandë is a known expat hub [unverified].

### Bulgaria (Plovdiv; also Sofia / Varna)

1. **Wealth tax:** "There are no net wealth/worth taxes in Bulgaria." https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/bulgaria/individual/other-taxes
2. **Inheritance tax:**
   - It is a municipal tax under the Local Taxes and Fees Act, arts. 29–43.
   - **The spouse and direct-line heirs (children, grandchildren) are fully exempt** under art. 31(2), whatever the value.
   - Other heirs pay tax only above €127,823 (BGN 250,000) per heir: 0.4–0.8% for siblings and their children, 3.3–6.6% for others.
   - **Scope:** Bulgarian nationals are taxed on assets worldwide, but **foreign nationals are taxed only on property in Bulgaria**. A US-citizen decedent's US portfolio is therefore out of scope, and the children are exempt in any case.
   - It is heir-based. There is no US estate treaty.
   - Sources: https://www.innovires.com/en/blog/danak-nasledstvo-bulgaria.html [secondary, law firm]. The minfin.bg/en/780 page returned 403, so the official confirmation is **[unverified]**.
3. **Tax on investment income:**
   - Residents are taxed on worldwide income at a 10% flat rate. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/bulgaria/individual/taxes-on-personal-income
   - **Dividends: 5% final**, whether Bulgarian or foreign. Foreign dividends with no Bulgarian withholding are self-assessed in the annual return.
   - Interest: **0% on EU/EEA bank accounts** since 1 Apr 2022; **10%** on accounts outside the EU/EEA.
   - Capital gains on shares: 10%, but **exempt** on EU/EEA regulated markets and, since 2021, on "equivalent third-country" markets.
   - Whether NYSE/Nasdaq count as "equivalent" is **[unverified]**. It matters little under BBD because nothing is sold.
   - Source for the three rows above: https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/bulgaria/individual/income-determination
   - The foreign tax credit is per country, and the US is a treaty partner. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/bulgaria/individual/foreign-tax-relief-and-tax-treaties
   - **[inference]** The US tax a citizen pays on US dividends will usually exceed Bulgaria's 5%, so the Bulgarian tax after credit may be nil. Check how the treaty's saving clause and resourcing rule work.
4. **Residency routes:**
   - **Pensioner D visa leading to a residence permit**, under art. 24(1)(10) of the Foreigners Act.
     - It needs a "right to a pension under Bulgarian or foreign law and sufficient means of subsistence". The benchmark is at least the minimum monthly wage (€620.20 in 2026) for the period of stay.
     - Apply in person at the consulate (interview; €100 fee; up to 35 working days). The D visa lasts up to 6 months, and the residence permit is then filed in Bulgaria for up to 1 year at a time.
     - The firm found **no ground for savings-only applicants** without a pension.
     - The investment route needs at least BGN 600,000 (€306,775) in Bulgarian real estate.
     - Source for the pensioner route: https://innovires.com/en/blog/viza-d-bulgaria.html [secondary, law firm]
   - Forum posts put the proof of funds at 6 months of minimum wage (~€3,721) for the visa and 12 months (~€7,442) for the permit [unverified, search snippet].
   - A US retiree with Social Security entitlement should fit the pension ground [inference].
5. **Healthcare:**
   - **NHIF (public):** self-insured people pay 8% of declared income. The minimum base is €620.20/month from 1 Aug 2026, so about **€50/month**; the maximum is about €184/month. Whether a non-working foreign pensioner *must* join is not settled in the source.
   - Non-EU permit holders need private cover of at least €30,000 (medical plus repatriation) as a condition of the permit. Local individual plans run €300–1,200/yr; no age bands are published.
   - Source for both points above: https://innovires.com/tax-residency/blog/bulgaria-health-insurance-foreigners.html [secondary]
   - **Hospitals (UK embassy list):**
     - Sofia: **Acibadem City Clinic University Hospital (JCI)** and Vita Hospital.
     - **Plovdiv: Eurohospital Plovdiv** (private, no accreditation listed).
     - Varna: Acibadem City Clinic Medical Centre and Eurohospital Varna (private), plus public St Marina / St Anna.
     - https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/bulgaria-list-of-medical-facilities/bulgaria-doctors
   - The Acibadem office in Plovdiv only coordinates patients; it is not a clinic. https://acibademinternational.com/international-offices/office-bulgaria-plovdiv/
   - Serious cases from Plovdiv go to Sofia, ~1.5–2 h away [unverified].
6. **Broker / margin:** Bulgaria is in the EU, so plan on IBKR margin. Schwab One International is not on the checked accepted list and reportedly bars Europe-resident customers from taking cash out through margin (Appendix E §1).

**BBD verdict:** Among the best tax profiles in the EU for BBD. There is no wealth tax, foreigners' foreign assets are outside inheritance tax and the children are exempt anyway, dividends pay 5% (often fully offset by credit for US tax), EU interest is 0%, and nothing is taxed until sold. The catch is residence: the easy route requires a pension (Social Security qualifies [inference]), and healthcare outside Sofia is middling.

**City notes:**
- **Safety (UK FCDO):** petty theft mainly in resorts (Sunny Beach). Protests are mostly peaceful. Isolated hostility toward LGBT+ people has been reported in Burgas and **Plovdiv**. Driving standards are poor. https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/bulgaria/safety-and-security
- **Climate [unverified]:** Plovdiv and Sofia are continental, with hot summers and cold winters; Sofia sits at ~550 m. Varna is Black Sea, with milder winters.
- **Flights:** **no Sofia–US nonstop**. A Bulgarian minister had expected GullivAir New York flights from Apr 2026, with no sign they launched [unverified, search snippets: sofiaglobe, BNR, Skyscanner]. In practice, connect via Vienna, Frankfurt, Istanbul and similar hubs.
- **Expat community [unverified]:** moderate in Sofia and Varna, smaller in Plovdiv. The UK retiree base is mainly rural and Black Sea.

### Austria (Vienna)

1. **Wealth tax:** "There is no wealth/worth tax in Austria." https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/austria/individual/other-taxes
2. **Inheritance tax:**
   - "There are no inheritance, estate, or gift taxes in Austria."
   - Real-property acquisition tax still applies to gratuitous transfers of Austrian real estate: 0.5% up to €250k, 2% from €250,001 to €400k, 3.5% above that.
   - Source for both points above: https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/austria/individual/other-taxes
   - **A US–Austria estate and gift tax treaty exists.** https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-gift-tax-treaties-international. With no Austrian death tax, the treaty is mostly moot; the US estate tax applies as usual.
   - Austria's gift-notification duty (Schenkungsmeldegesetz) was not checked **[unverified]**.
3. **Tax on investment income (KESt special rate):**
   - Dividends, interest on listed securities, and gains on securities bought after 31 Dec 2010: **27.5%**, as a final tax.
   - Bank deposit interest: **25%**.
   - Income on a **foreign** account such as IBKR or Schwab "must be included in the income tax return and is taxed at a 27.5% special tax rate".
   - Losses offset only within the same year and are never carried forward. Loan interest "cannot be offset against positive interest". Source for the rows above: https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/austria/individual/income-determination
   - The general ban on deducting expenses tied to income taxed at the special rate (§20(2) EStG) is **[unverified]**: the RIS page returned 503. In practice margin interest gives **no Austrian deduction**.
   - **Big BBD problem: non-reporting funds.**
     - Funds not registered with OeKB are taxed each year on a deemed distribution: "90% of the growth in value during the year", with a minimum of 10% of year-end value, at 27.5%. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/austria/individual/income-determination
     - **US-domiciled ETFs (VTI, VOO, etc.) are generally not OeKB reporting funds** **[unverified, inference]**. Guides tell EU residents to hold UCITS ETFs "not US funds like VTI". https://myetfjourney.com/blog/etf-investing-austria [secondary]
     - **A never-sell US-ETF portfolio would therefore be taxed every year at ~27.5% × 90% of its growth.** That defeats the "borrow" part of BBD.
     - Individual US stocks are not caught; they are taxed only on dividends and on sale.
     - The US-person PFIC rules make switching to UCITS ETFs costly as well [unverified].
   - Austria credits foreign tax under the US treaty. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/austria/individual/foreign-tax-relief-and-tax-treaties. **[inference]** Credit at the 15% treaty rate leaves about 12.5% Austrian tax on US dividends on top of the US tax.
4. **Residency route: Niederlassungsbewilligung – ausgenommen Erwerbstätigkeit** (settlement permit with no right to work; NAG §44):
   - **Income at 1 Jan 2026:**

     | Household | Regular net monthly income |
     |---|---|
     | Single | €2,616.78 |
     | Couple | €4,128.24 |
     | Each child | +€403.76 |

     Applicants can show "pension or pension payments, or sufficient personal assets".
   - Other requirements: health insurance covering all risks, local-standard accommodation, and **German proof with the first application**.
   - **Quota:** a quota place is needed for "other third-country nationals".
   - Apply in person **abroad** at an Austrian embassy before entry. The fee is €218.
   - Source for the points above: https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/menschen_aus_anderen_staaten/aufenthalt/3/2/Seite.120302
   - The Tyrol government confirms the "Privatiers" sub-quota is set each year in the Niederlassungsverordnung; no numbers given. https://www.tirol.gv.at/gesellschaft-soziales/einwanderung/welcheniederlassungsbewilligu
   - Vienna's 2026 quota is **[unverified]**. A search snippet said Vienna had received only 14 applications to 28 Nov 2025 for its 2025 quota, so the quota is probably not binding; the PDF could not be decoded.
   - Wien.gv.at says the permit lasts 12 months and is renewable, integration Module 1 must be done within 2 years, and permanent residence (Daueraufenthalt-EU) is possible after 5 years [search snippet; page not fetched, **unverified**].
5. **Healthcare:**
   - Once resident, the person can join the public **ÖGK self-insurance (Selbstversicherung)** at **€565.25/month (2026)**. A reduction is possible on application.
   - Benefits start after a **6-month waiting period** unless the person had recent ASVG cover. The page does not mention a health questionnaire or exclusion for pre-existing conditions.
   - Source for the two points above: https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/gesundheit/selbstversicherung_in_der_krankenversicherung
   - The permit itself needs all-risk private cover first. Cigna Global has no entry-age cap (Appendix E §3).
   - Vienna's hospitals (AKH Wien, the university hospital) are excellent [unverified, general knowledge].
6. **Broker / margin:** Austria is in the EU, so plan on IBKR. Schwab One International is not on the checked accepted list and reportedly bars Europe-resident customers from taking cash out through margin (Appendix E §1). An Austrian bank depot would withhold KESt automatically. A foreign broker means self-filing, including the deemed income on funds.

**BBD verdict:** No wealth, inheritance or gift tax and a US estate treaty, but 27.5% on dividends and, crucially, annual deemed-income tax on non-OeKB funds. That makes Austria poor for an ETF-based never-sell portfolio, and only middling for one of individual stocks. The residence permit (≈€2.6k/mo single, German, quota) is workable and healthcare is first-rate.

**City notes:**
- **Safety (UK FCDO):** "Crime levels are generally low"; there is pickpocketing in city centres. Terrorism: "Terrorists are likely to try to carry out attacks in Austria", citing Vienna 2020 and Villach 2025. https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/austria/safety-and-security
- **Climate [unverified]:** continental: warm summers, cold grey winters.
- **Flights:** Austrian Airlines' four North American destinations in winter 2025/26 were New York JFK, Washington Dulles, Boston and Chicago O'Hare. Boston and Chicago were paused from mid-January to February 2026. https://frankfurtflyer.de/?p=184875 [secondary]. Newark and seasonal LAX are reported but **unverified**. American Airlines plans Philadelphia–Vienna from May 2027 [unverified, search snippet].
- **Expat community [unverified]:** large international and UN community in Vienna; English widely spoken.

### Mauritius (Grand Baie / west coast)

1. **Wealth tax:** "There are no net wealth/worth taxes in Mauritius." https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/mauritius/individual/other-taxes
2. **Inheritance tax:** "There are no inheritance, estate, or gift taxes in Mauritius." https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/mauritius/individual/other-taxes. Registration duty on inherited Mauritian property was not checked [unverified]. There is no US estate treaty, and with no local tax none is needed.
3. **Tax on investment income (remittance basis):**
   - Residents are taxed on worldwide income, but "income derived from outside Mauritius is taxable only to the extent that it is received in Mauritius". https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/mauritius/individual/taxes-on-personal-income
   - **Rates from 1 Jul 2025:** 0% on the first MUR 500k, 10% on the next MUR 500k, 20% above that.
   - **Fair Share Contribution:** 15% on income above MUR 12m, for the year beginning 1 Jul 2025 and the following two. https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/mauritius/individual/taxes-on-personal-income
   - A secondary page shows a 35% band above MUR 12m under the "Finance Act 2026". The two may be the same thing presented differently. https://real-estate-mauritius.mu/en/retiring-in-mauritius/ [secondary]
   - **No capital gains tax.** Foreign capital gains are untaxed. A credit for foreign tax "normally nullifies any tax incidence in Mauritius on foreign income". https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/mauritius/individual/income-determination
   - **Premium Visa (official page):**
     - Spending on foreign credit or debit cards "will not be deemed to have been remitted".
     - Money deposited in a Mauritian bank is taxable, unless a declaration confirms the tax was paid in the country of origin or residence.
     - https://residency.mu/live/mauritius-premium-visa/
   - Tax residence starts at 183 days in the income year, or 270 days across that year and the two before. https://real-estate-mauritius.mu/en/retiring-in-mauritius/ [secondary, citing the MRA]

### South Africa (Cape Town)

1. **Wealth tax:** none. PwC (reviewed 29 May 2026) says: "South Africa does not levy any net wealth/worth taxes."
   - https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/south-africa/individual/other-taxes

2. **Inheritance and estate tax:** estate duty, charged on the decedent's estate.
   - **Rates (PwC):** R3.5M abatement (about US$200k), then 20% up to R30M dutiable (about US$1.7M) and 25% above that.
   - **Scope:** worldwide for anyone ordinarily resident at death. Standard Bank: "the market value of all assets – including offshore assets – of the individual resident are included". For non-residents, only South African assets are taxed (PwC).
   - **Main relief for an American who moves in, s4(e) of the Estate Duty Act:**
     - Foreign property is deductible if acquired "before he became ordinarily resident in the Republic for the first time" (SAIT compendium text).
     - A practitioner article says the profits and proceeds of such property also qualify [secondary, 2017].
     - So a portfolio already held on arrival may be largely outside estate duty. Shares bought later with new money are in scope. How this works for a portfolio rebalanced over decades is **[unverified]**.
   - **CGT at death too:** South Africa treats death as a disposal at market value, so capital gains tax is due as well. There is a one-off R300k exclusion, 40% of the gain is included in income, and the deceased's marginal rate applies, giving at most about 18% (40% × 45%). Assets to a surviving spouse are excluded. The CGT is deductible for estate duty [secondary: Capital Legacy; ASL].
   - **This defeats the US step-up** as far as South African tax goes: gains accrued while resident are taxed at death, and the US gives no credit, because it levies nothing on the gain at death. Whether pre-arrival gains get a market-value base cost on becoming resident (Eighth Schedule para 12) is **[unverified]**.
   - **Debts:** s4(b) allows debts due "to persons ordinarily resident within the Republic". Whether a margin loan from a US broker is deductible is **[unverified]**.
   - **US estate tax treaty: yes** (IRS list, estate only). Standard Bank says South Africa credits foreign duty on foreign-situs assets (situs of shares = place of incorporation), capped at the South African duty on them.
   - **Practical result:** below the US$15M US exemption there is no US estate tax to credit, so South African estate duty of 20–25% applies in full to any in-scope US shares.
   - https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/south-africa/individual/other-taxes
   - https://webtrading.standardbank.com/webtrader/downloads/Situs Tax Memo April 2016.pdf
   - https://jutamedia2.blob.core.windows.net/jutacoza/uploads/SAIT_Compenduim_2016_Volume1/files/basic-html/page668.html
   - https://wereldwyd.co.za/en/implications-estate-duty/
   - https://capitallegacy.co.za/faq/17/how-much-tax-do-you-pay-on-a-deceased-estate
   - https://www.asl.co.za/deceased-estates-and-the-various-tax-implications-applicable-on-death-2/
   - https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-gift-tax-treaties-international

3. **Dividends, interest and foreign income:** residents are taxed on worldwide income, with a top rate of 45% from R1,878,601 (2026/27).
   - **Foreign dividends:** "Most other foreign dividends are subject to tax at an effective rate of 20%."
   - **Interest:** only local interest gets an exemption: the first R23,800, or R34,500 at 65+. Foreign interest is taxed at marginal rates.
   - **Capital gains:** 40% of net gains are included in income.
   - **US–SA income treaty, Art. 23(2):** for a US citizen resident in South Africa, South Africa credits only the US tax a non-citizen would pay, which is 15% on portfolio dividends. So US dividends cost about 5% more in South Africa on top of that, and the US credits that residual. US-source interest, which the treaty exempts for non-citizens, gets no South African credit, so South Africa taxes it in full and the US then credits it.
   - **Exit tax, s9H:** ceasing South African residence triggers "a notional disposal of an individual's worldwide assets" on the day before, with South African immovable property excluded (PKF). For a BBD holder who later leaves, this realises the gains accrued while resident.
   - https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/south-africa/individual/taxes-on-personal-income
   - https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/south-africa/individual/income-determination
   - https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/safrica.pdf
   - https://pkf.co.za/news/2025/ceasing-south-african-tax-residency-key-tax-implications-for-emigrants/

4. **Residency on passive income:**
   - **Retired person visa (s20 of the Immigration Act):** the South African embassy in Buenos Aires notice (2026) sets a minimum of R37,000/mo (about US$2,100). It can come from a pension, irrevocable annuity or retirement account, "OR has a net worth OR a combination of assets realising a minimum monthly income". The visa lasts up to 4 years, is renewable, and permanent residence is available under s27(e).
   - **Financially independent permanent residence, s27(f):** an immigration consultancy (11 May 2026) [secondary] puts it at "typically requires assets of at least R12 million" (about US$690k), plus a one-off R120,000 payment after approval. The money need not be brought into the country (search summary **[unverified]**). The VFS and DHA primary pages were blocked or 404.
   - https://embajadasudafrica.org.ar/en/pdf/2026/Retired Person Visa - Section 20.pdf
   - https://www.ibn.co.za/blog-and-news/south-africa-fi-retirement-pr-faqs/

5. **Healthcare:**
   - **Quality:** FCDO: "South Africa has a very high standard of private medical care". Public care varies.
   - **Cape Town hospitals:** major private hospitals include Netcare Christiaan Barnard Memorial and Mediclinic Cape Town. These names come from search results only, as the expat-guide page was blocked **[unverified]**.
   - **Local medical schemes (open enrolment):** schemes cannot refuse an applicant on age. Instead they may load a late-joiner penalty on anyone joining at 35+ with no prior South African cover; foreign schemes don't count. The bands are 5%, 25%, 50%, or 75% of the contribution for 25+ uncovered years, which is what a new 70-year-old would pay (Discovery 2025 late-joiner document, read).
   - **Discovery Classic Comprehensive 2026:** R10,037/mo for the main member (medicalaid.com [secondary]). A 70-year-old would pay about R17,500/mo with the 75% loading, roughly US$1,000/mo or US$12k/yr. That is higher than Cigna Global's ~$6.5–9.4k (Appendix E), but cover is guaranteed with no entry-age cap.
   - **Foreign residents joining:** whether foreign residence-visa holders can join a South African scheme was not confirmed on a scheme page **[unverified]**.
   - https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/south-africa/health
   - https://lahealth.co.za/wcm/discoverycoza/assets/medical-aid/benefit-information/2025/late-joiner-penalty.pdf
   - https://medicalaid.com/medical-aid/discovery-health/comprehensive-series/classic-comprehensive/

6. **Broker and margin:**
   - **IBKR:** no South Africa-specific source found. Under Appendix E's general finding that IBKR serves non-sanctioned countries, the placement is "IBKR likely" **[unverified]**.
   - **Schwab One International:** South Africa is not in Appendix E's accepted list. Status unknown **[unverified]**.
   - **Local brokers:** South African brokers (e.g. Standard Bank Webtrader) offer US shares, but nothing found shows US-style margin lending against them.
   - https://webtrading.standardbank.com/webtrader/downloads/Situs Tax Memo April 2016.pdf

**BBD verdict:** Among the three, South Africa is the worst fit for BBD.
- **While alive:** worldwide income tax, with US dividends at about 20%.
- **At death:** CGT on gains accrued while resident, then 20–25% estate duty on a resident's worldwide estate.
- **On leaving:** an exit tax.
- **What helps:** the s4(e) exclusion for foreign assets held before arrival, and the US estate treaty, soften the estate side.
- **What doesn't:** nothing shelters the death-CGT on gains accrued while resident.

Cheap residency (R37k/mo, or R12M for permanent residence) and good private care don't offset that.

- `[unverified]` marks something I could not confirm from a page I read.
- I could not read US State Department pages directly (travel.state.gov returns HTTP 403). US levels come from traveladvisory.io, which mirrors them `[secondary]`; several of its country date stamps are old (1 Oct 2023).

### Hopkins (Belize)

- **Regional tax:** national rules apply. The QRP exempts foreign-source income (App. C, Belize).
- **Healthcare:**
  - The village has only a small public clinic; no facility in Hopkins is on the UK's medical list. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/belize-list-of-medical-facilities/belize-medical-facilities
  - **Nearest hospital: Southern Regional Hospital, Dangriga** (public). It offers general, maternity, ICU and surgical care and opened in 2000; belize.com calls it comparable to a North American community hospital. Chemotherapy is available at a small Dangriga clinic, but radiation means going abroad. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/belize-list-of-medical-facilities/belize-medical-facilities ; https://www.belize.com/medical-care-in-belize/
  - Dangriga is **about 20 miles (≈29 min)** by road. https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Hopkins-Belize/Dangriga
  - Private care is in Belize City (Belize Medical Associates, Belize Healthcare Partners), **about 2.5 h** away by expat reports `[secondary, search snippet only; expatexchange pages 403]`. https://www.belize.com/medical-care-in-belize/
  - No JCI hospital. Evacuation follows the national route (Belize City, then Mérida, Guatemala City or Miami; App. C).
- **Safety:**
  - US: **Level 2** (crime), updated 12 Mar 2026. Only Southside Belize City is Level 3. https://www.traveladvisory.io/advisory/belize `[secondary]`
  - UK: no area warnings for Hopkins. Flooded bridges and causeways in severe weather are flagged "particularly in Stann Creek and Toledo". https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/belize/safety-and-security
- **Retiree community and access:**
  - Village of 1,610 (2010 census), 70% Garifuna and 2.2% "Caucasian". No expat headcount was found; the community is small. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopkins,_Belize
  - English is the official language (App. C).
  - Airports: Dangriga airstrip about 30 min away (domestic flights to BZE; Placencia section, App. C). BZE has year-round US nonstops to ATL, DFW, MIA, IAH, HOU and LAX (App. C, Placencia).
- **Climate:** tropical rainforest (Af). It sits on the Caribbean shore in the hurricane belt; the village was founded in 1942 after a hurricane destroyed its predecessor. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopkins,_Belize
- **Costs:** no livingcost.org page (404). Numbeo could not be checked.
- **51–75?** **Borderline yes, low 70s.** It has Placencia's QRP tax profile, but a real regional hospital is about 30 min away, against Placencia's 1¼–1¾ h. Small, hurricane-exposed and basic.

### Corozal (Belize)

- **Regional tax:** national rules apply (QRP).
- **Healthcare:**
  - **Corozal Community Hospital** (public, secondary level) covers internal medicine, general surgery, paediatrics, obstetrics and gynaecology, and orthopaedics. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/belize-list-of-medical-facilities/belize-medical-facilities
  - The real draw is **Chetumal, Mexico, about 15.7 miles (≈26 min)** away (https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Corozal-Town/Chetumal). belize.com says retirees pick Corozal to be "closer to better health care across the border" and that many go to Chetumal for "a wider range of medical services" (Clínica Carranza is named); Northern Medical Plaza is recommended for northern Belize. https://www.belize.com/medical-care-in-belize/
  - No JCI hospital was found. Cross-border care needs a passport and border hours; insurer network coverage in Mexico is `[unverified]`.
  - Mérida is the next tier (App. C).
- **Safety:** US **Level 2** for Belize as above. The UK page does not mention Corozal or the Chetumal crossing. https://www.traveladvisory.io/advisory/belize `[secondary]` ; https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/belize/safety-and-security
- **Retiree community and access:**
  - Town of about 10–13.5k (2010 census; 2017 estimate). It is 84 miles north of Belize City and 9 miles from the Mexican border. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corozal_Town
  - Known as an expat and retiree town, but no headcount was found.
  - Corozal airstrip (CZH) has Tropic Air to San Pedro only. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corozal_Airport
  - Chetumal airport flies only to Mexico City, with **no US nonstops**. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chetumal_International_Airport
  - US nonstops are from BZE (App. C). The drive to BZE (≈2 h) is `[unverified]`.
- **Climate:** tropical savanna. Highs about 31.5 °C and about 1,355 mm of rain; June is wettest and February driest. Hurricane belt: **Hurricane Janet (1955) destroyed about 90% of the town.** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corozal_Town
- **Costs:** no livingcost.org page (404). Numbeo could not be checked.
- **51–75?** **Yes, 60s–70s.** QRP tax with the best hospital access in Belize outside Belize City and Cayo, because Mexican private care is 26 min away. Hurricane exposure and a 2 h drive to US flights count against it.

### Mazatlán (Mexico)

- **Regional tax:** national rules apply. Mexican states do not tax individuals' investment income as far as I know `[unverified]`; Sinaloa state taxes were not checked.
- **Healthcare:**
  - The city has private hospitals: **Hospital Marina Mazatlán**, Hospital Alhma Medical Center and Hospital Médica de la Ciudad (https://retireyoungtravelsmart.com/mazatlan-healthcare/ `[secondary, undated]`). Sharp, Bilbao and Worldclass also appear in search snippets `[unverified]`.
  - No JCI accreditation was found.
  - Major specialist referral goes to Guadalajara or Culiacán `[unverified]`.
- **Safety:**
  - UK: FCDO advises against all but essential travel to Sinaloa, **except "the cities of Los Mochis and Mazatlán"** and the 15D toll road. https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/mexico
  - US: Sinaloa is **Level 4 "Do Not Travel"** (May 2026 update), with Mazatlán listed at Level 4 and "tighter restrictions" for US government staff. The exact Mazatlán carve-out wording could not be read. https://latamtravellers.com/blogs/news/is-mexico-safe-in-2026-state-by-state-advisory `[secondary]` ; https://www.travelmarketreport.com/destinations/articles/us-updated-mexico-travel-advisory-2026-terrorism-crime-and-kidnapping `[secondary]`
  - The national level is 2 (29 May 2026). https://www.traveladvisory.io/advisory/mexico
- **Retiree community and access:**
  - City of about 442k (2020). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazatl%C3%A1n
  - It has a large US and Canadian snowbird and retiree community, but no fetched headcount `[unverified]`.
  - **MZT US nonstops:** LAX (Alaska), DFW and PHX (American), IAH (United), and seasonal MSP (Delta, Sun Country). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazatl%C3%A1n_International_Airport
- **Climate:** tropical savanna (Aw). Highs run from 25 °C (Jan) to 33 °C (Aug) with about 800 mm of rain, mostly July–September (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazatl%C3%A1n). Pacific hurricane exposure: **Willa (Cat 3, Oct 2018)** made landfall south of the city and forced 30 neighbourhoods to evacuate and the airport to close. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Willa
- **Costs:** https://livingcost.org/cost/mexico/mazatlan exists. Numbeo could not be checked.
- **51–75?** **Unlikely.** It carries Mexico's tax profile (worldwide income, 10% dividend tax; already ranked via PV, Mérida and SMA), and the US Level 4 state rating is a hard flag for many insurers and retirees. Include only if Mexico needs a Pacific beach city other than PV.

- **Healthcare:**

Public: Salvatierra (Secretariat of Health), IMSS, ISSSTE and naval hospitals. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Paz,_Baja_California_Sur ; https://owl-falcon-pc56.squarespace.com/medical-options-for-foreigners `[secondary]`
  - Some specialist treatment is unavailable, so residents go to Guadalajara (same squarespace source). A new private "Fifty Doctors" hospital is reported, but its status is `[unverified]`.
  - No JCI. Los Cabos hospitals are about 2 h away `[unverified]`.
- **Safety:**
  - UK: Baja California Sur is "unaffected by these warnings". https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/mexico
  - US: BCS **Level 2**, "exercise increased caution" (terrorism, crime), per the May 2026 update. https://www.travelmarketreport.com/destinations/articles/us-updated-mexico-travel-advisory-2026-terrorism-crime-and-kidnapping `[secondary]`
- **Retiree community and access:**
  - City of about 250k (2020). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Paz,_Baja_California_Sur
  - INM counts 19,765 foreigners resident in all of BCS (Feb 2024), with no municipal split. https://www.posta.com.mx/mexico/aumento-de-extranjeros-en-bcs-gentrificacion-a-la-vista/vl1543433 `[secondary]`
  - The older figure of about 3,500 US citizens in La Paz is from a forum and was not read.
  - **LAP US nonstops: only LAX (Alaska)**, about 11.4k passengers in 2025. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_M%C3%A1rquez_de_Le%C3%B3n_International_Airport SJD (≈2 h) has many more US routes `[unverified]`.
- **Climate:** hot desert (BWh). About 185 mm of rain, peaking Aug–Sep; summer highs 34–36 °C and winters 20–25 °C. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Paz,_Baja_California_Sur Exposed to Pacific hurricanes: **Odile (Cat 3, Sep 2014)** damaged the harbour, a stadium and the airport. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Odile
- **Costs:** https://livingcost.org/cost/mexico/la-paz exists (title "La Paz, Mexico"; the page names Baja California Sur). Numbeo could not be checked.
- **51–75?** **Yes, 60s–70s.** It is the safest-rated Mexican coast here (US L2, no UK warning), but has Mexican tax, mid-tier hospitals and one US nonstop.

### Tavira (Portugal)

- **Regional tax:** national rules apply (App. A).
- **Healthcare:**
  - "Tavira has no 24-hour urgency unit." The nearest is at **Vila Real de Santo António, about 30 min east**.
  - Hospitals are in Faro (28 km, about 30–40 min): public **Hospital de Faro** and private **Hospital CUF Faro (Gambelas, formerly HPA), described as JCI-accredited** `[secondary; JCI not checked on jointcommission.org]`.
  - Locally there is Centro de Saúde de Tavira (public, with after-hours cover) and the private Clínica do Plaza.
  - https://expatsi.com/cities/portugal/tavira `[secondary]` ; distance from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tavira
- **Safety:** US **Level 1** (updated 23 Dec 2025). https://www.traveladvisory.io/advisory/portugal `[secondary]`
- **Retiree community and access:**
  - Municipality of about 26k (2011). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tavira
  - The foreign community is "older and settled, mostly British, French and German", and English-speaking lawyers and doctors are easy to find. https://expatsi.com/cities/portugal/tavira
  - FAO is 35–40 min away and has **one US nonstop: United Newark (seasonal)**; 10.4M passengers in 2025. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faro_Airport
- **Climate:** hot-summer Mediterranean, about 3,150 h of sun, about 18 °C mean and about 480 mm of rain (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tavira ; expatsi). No hurricanes. Historic earthquake and tsunami risk (1755). Summer wildfire risk in the interior is `[unverified]`.
- **Costs:** https://livingcost.org/cost/portugal/tavira exists. Numbeo could not be checked.
- **51–75?** **Yes, 50s–60s.** Same tax case as the Algarve already ranked, with a quieter town, private JCI care 35 min away and a seasonal US nonstop. The main drawback is no 24 h emergency department in town.

### Torrevieja (Spain)

- **Regional tax:** **Valencian Community rules, identical to Valencia and Alicante.**
  - Wealth tax exempt minimum **€2,000,000** (Ley 5/2026, assessment date 31-12-2026), with no general relief and rates of 0.25–3.5%. https://guiafiscal.es/opinion/patrimonio-2026-mapa-bonificaciones-ccaa/
  - Inheritance: 99% relief for children (App. A and the Alicante section).
  - The national solidarity tax applies above €3M (App. A).
- **Healthcare:**
  - **Hospital Quirónsalud Torrevieja** (private) **renewed JCI accreditation** (press release 29 Jun 2026, JCI 8th edition, first accredited 2023). https://euroweeklynews.com/2026/06/30/quironsalud-torrevieja-hospital-renews-its-joint-commission-international-gold-seal-reinforcing-its-leadership-in-healthcare-quality/
  - This upgrades App. H's Alicante note from secondary to read.
  - The public Hospital Universitario de Torrevieja is in town `[unverified, not fetched]`.
- **Safety:** US **Level 2** (terrorism, civil unrest; updated 1 Aug 2026). https://www.traveladvisory.io/advisory/spain `[secondary]`
- **Retiree community and access:**
  - About 98.5k residents (1 Jan 2025). In 2012 Spanish nationals were 59.9%, with British and Russian residents each 5.6%. It was reportedly the municipality with the most British expats in Spain (2004, citation needed). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrevieja
  - It is the biggest English-speaking retiree enclave on this list.
  - ALC is **26.7 mi (≈42 min)** away (https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Torrevieja/Alicante-Airport-ALC) and has **no US nonstops** (App. H, Alicante).
- **Climate:** hot semi-arid (BSh), about 328 mm of rain with October wettest, and July–Aug highs about 32 °C. No hurricanes. Autumn DANA flash floods are a regional risk `[unverified]`. The 1829 earthquake levelled the town. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrevieja
- **Costs:** https://livingcost.org/cost/spain/torrevieja exists. Numbeo could not be checked.
- **51–75?** **Yes, 50s.** It is Alicante's tax profile, with a JCI hospital in town and a huge English-speaking community. It is cheaper, more built-up and less "city" than Alicante, and has no US nonstops.

### Hoi An (Vietnam)

- **Regional tax:** national rules apply. Administrative note: Hội An city was dissolved in 2025 and merged into Da Nang municipality. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%E1%BB%99i_An_(city)
- **Healthcare:**
  - Serious care is in Da Nang: Pacific Hospital (about 30 min) and Vinmec Da Nang.
  - **Vinmec Da Nang is not JCI-accredited** (only Vinmec Times City in Hanoi and Central Park in HCMC are; Da Nang has a 4-star GHR rating). https://www.vinmec.com/eng/medical-tourism/
  - Complex cases go to HCMC, Bangkok or Singapore (search snippets) `[secondary]`.
- **Safety:**
  - US **Level 1**, but the stamp is dated 2023. https://www.traveladvisory.io/advisory/vietnam `[secondary]`
  - UK: no area warnings for Hoi An. "Seasonal flooding occurs in October and November in central Vietnam"; tropical cyclones hit the east coast. https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/vietnam/safety-and-security
- **Retiree community and access:**
  - Former city of about 152k (2018). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%E1%BB%99i_An_(city) There is a known expat community, but no headcount was found.
  - DAD is 28 km (≈40 min) away (same source) and has **no US nonstops**; connect via Seoul, Tokyo, Taipei, Hong Kong, Singapore or Bangkok. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Da_Nang_International_Airport
- **Climate:** tropical monsoon. The rainy season (Sep–Jan) floods the old town regularly. In **2025 there were three floods in three weeks**, and on 28 Oct 2025 riverside streets were under up to 2.5 m of water, the highest at the Pagoda Bridge since 1999. https://e.vnexpress.net/photo/environment/hoi-an-floods-for-3rd-time-in-3-weeks-4965763.html Typhoon-exposed.
- **Costs:** https://livingcost.org/cost/vietnam/hoi-an exists. Numbeo could not be checked.
- **51–75?** **No.** Vietnam has no retirement visa (as with Da Nang and HCMC, excluded), plus severe annual flooding and no JCI hospital in the region.

### Chiang Rai (Thailand)

- **Regional tax:** national rules apply (App. D: LTR exempts foreign income; the remittance rules otherwise).
- **Healthcare:**
  - Hospitals: Overbrook (1903, mission origin), Kasemrad Sriburin, Chiang Rai Inter (private), **Bangkok Hospital Chiang Rai** (private) and Chiang Rai Prachanukroh (public). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Rai
  - Overbrook claims to be the first JCI-accredited hospital in Chiang Rai, with no year given. https://arokago.com/providers/overbrook-hospital `[secondary; not checked on jointcommission.org]`
  - Specialist care is often in Chiang Mai (about 200 km, about 3.5 h by bus). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Rai
- **Safety:**
  - US **Level 1** (2023 stamp). https://www.traveladvisory.io/advisory/thailand `[secondary]`
  - UK warnings cover only the deep south and the Cambodia border, not Chiang Rai. https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/thailand/warnings-and-insurance
- **Retiree community and access:**
  - City of about 70k (municipal district about 200k). It is smaller than Chiang Mai's expat scene; no headcount was found. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Rai
  - CEI is domestic to Bangkok (DMK and BKK) and Phuket, with Scoot to Singapore listed. **No US nonstops.** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mae_Fah_Luang_%E2%80%93_Chiang_Rai_International_Airport
- **Climate:** tropical wet-and-dry; April highs about 34.5 °C and about 1,723 mm of rain (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Rai). Hazards:
  - **Burning-season smoke:** AQI 269 on 2 Apr 2026, the highest in Thailand that day and worse than Chiang Mai. https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40064573
  - **Typhoon Yagi floods (Sep 2024):** at least 36 deaths in Chiang Rai province and the airport closed. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Yagi
- **Costs:** https://livingcost.org/cost/thailand/chiang-rai exists. Numbeo could not be checked.
- **51–75?** **Yes, 60s–70s.** Thai tax and LTR profile, cheap, with decent private hospitals. Smoke season is as bad as Chiang Mai's and there are fewer flights and specialists.

### Langkawi (Malaysia)

- **Regional tax:** **income tax is national** (foreign income exempt to 2036; App. D).
  - Langkawi is a **duty-free island (since 1987)**, which affects customs and excise on goods, not income tax. https://www.kosmo.com.my/?p=586379
  - MM2H's state property floor for Kedah is `[unverified]` (KL's is RM1m; App. D).
- **Healthcare:**
  - **Hospital Sultanah Maliha** (public) is "the only hospital on the island", 7 km from Kuah. https://www.penang-traveltips.com/malaysia/kedah/langkawi/hospital-langkawi.htm
  - **No private or specialist hospital** (Apr 2024). The tourism industry is still lobbying for one; non-emergency waits can be months, and foreigners pay foreigner rates (App. D). https://www.kosmo.com.my/?p=586379
  - Private care means Penang (Island, Gleneagles, Pantai, Adventist), a short AirAsia or Firefly flight away. No ferry to Penang is listed. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkawi_International_Airport ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkawi
- **Safety:** US **Level 1** (22 Feb 2026); only eastern Sabah is higher. https://www.traveladvisory.io/advisory/malaysia `[secondary]`
- **Retiree community and access:**
  - About 65k people on the main island, and 9.7% non-Malaysians in 2010. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkawi
  - There is a small expat retiree community, no headcount found. English is widely used in Malaysia (App. D).
  - LGK flies to KL, Penang, JB, Singapore, Dubai and Bengaluru; **no US nonstops**. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkawi_International_Airport
- **Climate:** tropical monsoon, more than 2,400 mm of rain; dry Dec–Feb and September wettest. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkawi It is outside the typhoon belt `[unverified]`.
- **Costs:** https://livingcost.org/cost/malaysia/langkawi exists. Numbeo could not be checked.
- **51–75?** **Borderline, low 70s.** Malaysian tax is the region's best, but there is no private hospital on the island, so it is a weaker Penang.

### Pedasí (Panama)

- **Regional tax:** national rules apply (territorial; App. C).
- **Healthcare:**
  - Pedasí has a public health centre and a small Minsa-Capsi public hospital.
  - **Las Tablas public hospital is about 30 min** away and **Chitré (Hospital Gustavo Nelson Collado, the Azuero hub) about 1 h**. https://www.panamaequity.com/blogs/health-insurance-and-medical-care-pedasi-panama/ `[secondary, 2019]`
  - Private clinics are in Las Tablas and Chitré `[secondary]`.
  - Serious care is in Panama City (Punta Pacífica, a Johns Hopkins affiliate; App. C), **about 5 h 20 min by road**. https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Pedas%C3%AD/Tocumen-International-Airport-PTY
  - No JCI hospital nearby.
- **Safety:**
  - US **Level 2**; only parts of Darién and the Mosquito Gulf are Level 4 (2023 stamp). https://www.traveladvisory.io/advisory/panama `[secondary]`
  - UK: no area warnings for Los Santos. **9 Oct 2026: M7.6 earthquake at 10 km depth, about 39 km west of Tonosí** (Los Santos, the same peninsula as Pedasí). Tsunami warnings were lifted and damage was still being assessed. https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/panama
- **Retiree community and access:**
  - A small surf and fishing town; no expat headcount was found.
  - PTY is about 5 h 20 min by car (bus about 8 h) and has about **19 US nonstop cities** on Copa and US carriers. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tocumen_International_Airport
- **Climate:** tropical, with a dry season. The UK says hurricanes don't directly hit Panama but bring heavy rain (wettest Oct–Nov). https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/panama/safety-and-security Earthquake-exposed, as above.
- **Costs:** no livingcost.org page (404). Numbeo could not be checked.
- **51–75?** **No.** Tax is excellent, but it is 5+ h from serious care and US flights, the area has only basic public hospitals, and there was a fresh M7.6 nearby. Coronado (App. H) is the better Panama beach pick.

### Volcán (Panama)

- **Regional tax:** national rules apply (territorial).
- **Healthcare:**
  - Volcán's hospital (opened 2014) is "a basic emergency care facility". Concepción is about 30 min away and **David's private hospitals (Hospital Chiriquí, Mae Lewis) about 40 min–1 h**. https://internationalliving.com/countries/panama/volcan-panama `[secondary, 2018]`
  - Specialist care means flying to Panama City (App. C, Boquete).

  - The expat community is "just around 250 people" (2018). https://internationalliving.com/countries/panama/volcan-panama `[secondary, dated]`

  - US flights are via PTY (19 US cities). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tocumen_International_Airport

- **Costs:** no livingcost.org page (404). Numbeo could not be checked.
- **51–75?** **Borderline, 70s.** It is a cheaper, smaller Boquete (already ranked) with the same David hospitals a bit further away. It adds little beyond Boquete.

- **Safety:**
  - US Ecuador **Level 2** (18 Aug 2026), with Imbabura not listed among higher-risk areas. https://www.traveladvisory.io/advisory/ecuador `[secondary]`
  - UK: Imbabura is not in any warning zone. https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ecuador/warnings-and-insurance
- **Retiree community and access:**
  - Town of 10.5k (2022) at 2,418 m; "resident foreigners totaled more than 500". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotacachi_(city)

  - City of 35k; dry (BWh, about 111 mm of rain). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salinas,_Ecuador
  - No livingcost.org page.
  - **Not ranked.**
- **51–75?** **No for both.** Ecuador's worldwide income tax and 35% inheritance tax on foreign assets are already the reason Cuenca is low. Salinas also carries UK "all but essential" and US Level 3 advice.

### Lake Atitlán: Panajachel / San Marcos La Laguna (Guatemala)

- **Regional tax:** national rules apply. Guatemala is territorial, its inheritance tax was repealed in April 2026, and it levies only the municipal IUSI property tax (App. F).
- **Healthcare:**
  - **Panajachel has no hospital**, only the public CAP Panajachel (24 h per the MSPAS registry) and private clinics (Pana Med, Centro Clínico Panajachel) whose hours are unconfirmed.
  - Hospital Nacional de Sololá (public) is about 20 min uphill `[secondary snippet]`.
  - Hospitalito Atitlán (Santiago Atitlán, across the lake) is a non-profit with 24 h emergency care. https://livinginguatemala.com/directories/hospitals-panajachel/
  - Specialist care is **about 3 h away in Guatemala City** (or 2 h to Xela), and helicopter evacuation is sometimes used. https://livinginguatemala.com/panajachel/ `[secondary]`
  - **San Marcos depends on Panajachel by boat**, and public boats stop at 16:30–17:00. https://livinginguatemala.com/directories/hospitals-panajachel/
  - No JCI hospital.
- **Safety:**
  - US Guatemala **Level 3, "Reconsider Travel"** (stamp 2023). Level 4 covers the **San Marcos Department** (a different place from San Marcos La Laguna, which is in Sololá), Huehuetenango, Zone 18 and Villa Nueva. https://www.traveladvisory.io/advisory/guatemala `[secondary]`
  - UK: no area warning for Sololá, but "armed attacks or robberies on most tourist routes between Guatemala City, Antigua … and Lake Atitlán". https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/guatemala/safety-and-security ; https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/guatemala/warnings-and-insurance
  - Locally: occasional night robberies on the Panajachel–Sololá road, and a lancha capsize killed two tourists in Jan 2026. https://livinginguatemala.com/panajachel/ `[secondary]`
- **Retiree community and access:**
  - Panajachel has 15k people (2018) at 1,597 m (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panajachel) and "a 50-year history of foreign residents", with no headcount (https://livinginguatemala.com/panajachel/).
  - San Marcos La Laguna is a village of about 2.2–2.9k. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Marcos_La_Laguna
  - GUA is about 3 h away and has broad US nonstops (LAX, JFK, EWR, MIA, FLL, ATL, DFW, IAH, ORD, IAD seasonal and others). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Aurora_International_Airport
- **Climate:** mild highland (Aw bordering Cwa), highs about 25 °C year-round and about 1,490 mm of rain (June wettest). Mudslide-exposed: **Hurricane Stan (Oct 2005)** destroyed about 100 Panajachel homes. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panajachel The UK warns of rainy-season landslides and volcano risk. https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/guatemala/safety-and-security
- **Costs:** https://livingcost.org/cost/guatemala/panajachel exists. There is no San Marcos page. Numbeo could not be checked.
- **51–75?** **Panajachel borderline (70s); San Marcos no.** Tax is excellent, but the nearest real hospital is 3 h away and the US rates Guatemala Level 3; Antigua carries Guatemala better. San Marcos adds boat-only access to the same gap.
